### Introduction

Between July and August 2026, we observed multiple suspicious Japanese-language emails disguised as reports of damaged products or refund requests. The emails asked recipients to check the condition of a product and directed them to URLs impersonating document-sharing or video-viewing websites. At the destination URL, a message stated that the file could not be previewed because of its large size and prompted the recipient to download a ZIP file.

During the same period, we also observed suspicious Korean-language emails with similar subject lines and body text. In addition, the destination pages provided language buttons for Vietnamese, English, Chinese, Japanese, and Korean. This suggests that similar emails in other languages may have been distributed to target countries across East and Southeast Asia.

We identified multiple similar emails associated with this campaign, but the downloaded ZIP files had a different structure in each case. The archives generally contained an EXE file with a double extension and a DLL file. Although the payloads belonged to the Pure malware family, specifically PureRAT or PureLogs [1](#fn:1), the loader implementation varied between samples. Some archives also included a Python interpreter or BootRepair.sys, a Lenovo-signed driver.

This article describes the attack flow from suspicious email to malware infection and presents our analysis of each stage.
