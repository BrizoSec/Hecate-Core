## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Executive impersonation and invoice fraud using AI involves attackers leveraging advanced machine learning models to mimic the writing style of high-level executives in an organization. This allows them to send convincing emails or messages requesting financial transactions such as wire transfers or payments, often targeting the finance department or other key personnel.

### Key Findings
- AI-assisted executive impersonation and invoice fraud relies on sophisticated machine learning models trained on large datasets of corporate communication patterns.
- The primary goal is to deceive employees into initiating unauthorized financial transactions by mimicking legitimate business communications.
- Organizations must implement robust verification mechanisms, such as secondary authentication for wire transfers and payments, to mitigate the risk of these types of fraud.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are increasingly using AI to impersonate executives in organizations for the purpose of conducting invoice fraud, where they leverage sophisticated technology to mimic the communication style and authority of senior leaders in order to manipulate employees into transferring funds or sensitive information. This method exploits trust within an organization and relies on the inability to detect subtle nuances in AI-generated communications.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries use advanced machine learning models to generate convincing impersonations of executives, enabling them to issue fraudulent instructions that appear legitimate.
- [UNCERTAIN] The specific tools used by adversaries for such attacks are not specified; however, it is reasonable to assume they involve sophisticated AI platforms capable of language and context understanding.
- Organizations should be wary of unexpected requests for financial transactions or sensitive information via email or messaging systems, as these can often be a telltale sign of executive impersonation fraud.
- [UNCERTAIN] Indicators may include unusual urgency in communications, requests to bypass normal approval processes, and discrepancies between the tone of messages and established communication patterns.
