## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Government networks are experiencing a structural shift in attack vectors, with compromised identities via phishing surging to 23% of intrusions, enabling adversaries to mimic legitimate activity and extend dwell times. The rapid weaponization of vulnerabilities (under 24 hours) and the interconnected nature of government ecosystems require hunt teams to prioritize behavioral anomalies in identity and process execution over static signature detection.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use compromised identities and legitimate activity mimicry to establish persistent access and lateral movement within interconnected government networks, evading early detection.

### Gaps Identified
- Lack of telemetry correlating user behavior analytics (UEBA) with process execution chains during the initial identity compromise phase, specifically failing to detect 'living-off-the-land' binaries used immediately after phishing success.
- Insufficient monitoring of cross-domain trust relationships between government agencies and third-party contractors, allowing attackers to pivot laterally across network boundaries that traditional perimeter defenses do not inspect.

### Key Findings
