## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
The Akira ransomware attack chain involves an adversary leveraging Remote Desktop Protocol for initial access, followed by antivirus evasion and the deployment of legitimate utilities like Rclone and GOST tunneling tools to facilitate data exfiltration and maintain persistence. The malware execution is marked by the disabling of system shadow copies via PowerShell and the launch of a malicious executable masquerading as svchost.exe from the C:\PerfLogs\temp directory.

### Key Findings
- The ransomware binary (SHA256: d00833318a04caa019c6f95dcb3598ad947d405010bfb2f3cbd04530a00bc3a4) was disguised as svchost.exe and located in C:\PerfLogs\temp, a path often used for obfuscation, while the GOST tunneling tool (SHA256: 1f1bb322591b6d27fd2946e373d7d2efc2f4e1e66818846060d391600b600fba) utilized C:\storage\win.exe.
- The adversary employed Rclone from the C:\PerfLogs folder for data exfiltration and established persistence through a GOST tunnel connecting to the Command and Control server at IP 64.227.4.134 (hosted on DigitalOcean).
- Preparation for encryption included the execution of PowerShell commands to retrieve and remove Windows Volume Shadow Copies using Get-WmiObject and Remove-WmiObject, effectively preventing volume restoration.
- VirusTotal analysis identified the GOST tunneling executable as 'pua.gost/golly' with known filenames including gost-windows-amd64.exe and TdAgent.exe, indicating its use for encrypted tunneling rather than standard service hosting.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
The Akira ransomware group gains initial access via Remote Desktop Protocol (RDP), disables antivirus protections, and uses Rclone for data exfiltration before executing the ransomware payload. Post-encryption, they deploy a GOST tunneling tool to maintain persistence within the network.

### Key Findings
- Initial compromise was achieved through Remote Desktop Protocol (RDP) followed by the disabling of antivirus protections on the endpoint.
- The adversary utilized Rclone launched from C:\PerfLogs for data exfiltration and a GOST tunneling tool executed after encryption to establish persistence.
- The ransomware binary (SHA256: 1f1bb322591b6d27fd2946e373d7d2efc2f4e1e66818846060d391600b600fba) was identified at C:\storage\win.exe.
- The GOST tunnel executable (SHA256: d00833318a04caa019c6f95dcb3598ad947d405010bfb2f3cbd04530a00bc3a4) was located at C:\PerfLogs\temp\svchost.exe and communicated with C2 IP 64.227.4.134.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
The Akira ransomware campaign leverages RDP for initial access and employs legitimate utilities like Rclone and a GOST tunneling executable masquerading as svchost.exe to exfiltrate data and establish persistence. The attack chain is characterized by the deletion of Volume Shadow Copies via PowerShell WMI commands and the execution of payloads from non-standard directories such as C:\PerfLogs\temp, communicating with a DigitalOcean-hosted C2 infrastructure.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use GOST tunneling tools disguised as svchost.exe in system logs combined with Rclone for data exfiltration to maintain persistence and avoid detection on compromised Windows endpoints.

### Gaps Identified
- Lack of specific signature or behavioral detection for the 'pua.gost/golly' classification and unknown hash 1f1bb322591b6d27fd2946e373d7d2efc2f4e1e66818846060d391600b600fba, which may evade traditional AV due to lack of widespread VT hits.
- Insufficient monitoring for anomalous process execution chains where legitimate tools (powershell.exe, rclone.exe) are launched from non-standard paths like C:\PerfLogs\temp or C:\storage without associated whitelisted parent processes.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use GOST tunneling tools disguised as svchost.exe in system logs combined with Rclone for data exfiltration to maintain persistence and avoid detection on compromised Windows endpoints.
- Gap: Lack of specific signature or behavioral detection for the 'pua.gost/golly' classification and unknown hash 1f1bb322591b6d27fd2946e373d7d2efc2f4e1e66818846060d391600b600fba, which may evade traditional AV due to lack of widespread VT hits.
- Gap: Insufficient monitoring for anomalous process execution chains where legitimate tools (powershell.exe, rclone.exe) are launched from non-standard paths like C:\PerfLogs\temp or C:\storage without associated whitelisted parent processes.
- Focus: Deploy telemetry to detect outbound encrypted traffic patterns consistent with GOST tunneling protocols towards known malicious IPs (e.g., 64.227.4.134) and monitor for WMI-based shadow copy deletion events followed immediately by ransomware-like file encryption activity.
