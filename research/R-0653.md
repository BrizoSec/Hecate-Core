## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
The modern Security Operations Center (SOC) operates by integrating telemetry across diverse environments such as endpoints, cloud workloads, SaaS applications, and identity systems to reconstruct complete attack paths. This approach shifts the focus from static indicators to behavioral analysis, allowing teams to investigate cross-environment pivots where activity spans multiple attack surfaces.

### Key Findings
- A significant portion of modern attacks (43% in 2026) involve activity across four or more distinct attack surfaces, necessitating the correlation of data across endpoints, cloud, SaaS, and identity domains.
- SOCs address fragmented visibility by employing behavioral models that compare events against baselines, such as analyzing suspicious PowerShell commands streamed to a SIEM within seconds for immediate evaluation.
- Operational response involves predefining containment actions for specific scenarios like compromised accounts or unauthorized remote access to enable rapid threat containment before business impact occurs.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries exploit visibility gaps created by disconnected security tools to pivot across the enterprise environment after establishing an initial foothold. This cross-environment movement involves lateral movement, persistence, and impact, requiring attackers to navigate between disparate systems that lack integrated monitoring.

### Key Findings
- Cross-environment attacks are driven by visibility gaps resulting from disconnected security tools within the enterprise architecture.
- Defense requires connecting evidence across domains through lateral movement, persistence, and impact phases of the attack lifecycle.
- Effective mitigation relies on cross-domain correlation to provide context for investigating complete attack paths.
- Automated incident storyline correlation is necessary to assist SOC analysts in responding effectively to multi-stage threats.
- Understanding Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTPs) is essential for detecting and disrupting threat activity across different environments.
- Security operations must undergo continuous testing and evolution to keep pace with the evolving nature of cross-environment pivoting.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Modern adversaries exploit visibility gaps between disconnected security tools to pivot across endpoints, cloud, SaaS, and identity domains, with 43% of attacks spanning four or more attack surfaces. Effective detection requires shifting from static indicators to behavioral correlation that connects lateral movement and persistence artifacts across these disparate environments. Hunt teams must prioritize correlating suspicious activity streams, such as PowerShell commands, against baselines to reconstruct complete cross-environment kill chains.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use cross-environment pivoting across endpoints, cloud, SaaS, and identity systems to evade isolated detection controls and establish persistent access.

### Gaps Identified
- Lack of integrated telemetry correlation between identity providers and endpoint/cloud workloads allows attackers to pivot across domains without triggering unified behavioral alerts.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use cross-environment pivoting across endpoints, cloud, SaaS, and identity systems to evade isolated detection controls and establish persistent access.
- Gap: Lack of integrated telemetry correlation between identity providers and endpoint/cloud workloads allows attackers to pivot across domains without triggering unified behavioral alerts.
- Focus: Correlate suspicious PowerShell execution events in SIEM with concurrent identity authentication logs and cloud workload API calls to identify lateral movement patterns.
- Focus: Implement automated storyline correlation to detect rapid sequences of actions that span multiple security domains, enabling faster containment before business impact occurs.
