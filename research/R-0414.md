## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are likely to abuse the Maltrail system by leveraging its public nature and focus on malware IPs and domains as a way to evade detection or mask their true activities. They may attempt to disrupt or manipulate the Maltrail data feed, either to hide their own malicious infrastructure or to sow confusion among defenders.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries will use domain generation algorithms (DGAs) to generate large numbers of potential command and control (C2) server addresses that are not yet flagged by Maltrail, allowing them to establish C2 communications without immediate detection.
- [UNCERTAIN] Adversaries may attempt to create false positives in the Maltrail system by sending benign traffic from clean IPs to known malicious domains or IP addresses on specific dates like 2026-08-01 to disrupt analysis and confuse defenders.
- The use of fast-flux networks will be prevalent, where adversaries rapidly change the IP address associated with a domain, making it difficult for Maltrail to maintain accurate lists of compromised systems.
- Adversaries might exploit time-zone differences or daylight saving changes to synchronize malicious activities with updates in the Maltrail database feed, timing their attacks when detection is least likely.
- Sophisticated adversaries could employ techniques such as DNS tunneling and data exfiltration through legitimate channels that mimic benign network activity not currently monitored by Maltrail's indicators.
- [UNCERTAIN] There might be an increase in the use of peer-to-peer (P2P) botnets, which inherently lack a centralized C2 infrastructure targeted by Maltrail's monitoring techniques.
