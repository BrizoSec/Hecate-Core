## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Credential attacks involve attackers leveraging stolen or weak credentials to gain unauthorized access to systems. Mitigating such attacks typically involves implementing strong authentication mechanisms, monitoring and analyzing user behavior for anomalies that suggest compromised credentials, and responding quickly to detected threats.

### Key Findings
- Strong password policies and multi-factor authentication (MFA) are critical in preventing the success of credential attacks.
- [UNCERTAIN] The exact frequency or scale of large-scale credential breaches is not specified but such incidents often lead to significant data compromises.
- Monitoring for unusual login patterns, such as multiple failed attempts from different IP addresses, can help identify and mitigate credential abuse.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries often exploit weak or stolen credentials through various methods such as phishing and brute force attacks to gain unauthorized access to systems. They may use these compromised credentials for lateral movement, data exfiltration, and maintaining persistent access.

### Key Findings
- Credential stuffing is a common technique where attackers reuse stolen login information across multiple platforms.
- [UNCERTAIN] Automated tools are used by adversaries to test large numbers of credential combinations against websites and services.
- Monitoring for unusual login patterns from suspicious IP addresses can help detect potential credential abuse early on.
- Implementing multi-factor authentication (MFA) is a key defense mechanism against credential-based attacks.
