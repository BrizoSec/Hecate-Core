## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Sandworm targeted Centreon systems over a period of four years from late 2017 to 2020, using webshells like P.A.S. and Exaramel for backdoor access. The campaign included exfiltration activities conducted through existing C2 channels.

### Key Findings
- Sandworm used webshells such as P.A.S. and Exaramel to establish a persistent presence on Centreon servers.
- The intrusion lasted from late 2017 until 2020, indicating prolonged operational security (OPSEC) measures by the attackers.
- Indicators of compromise related to Sandworm's activities were published for detection purposes.
- Backdoor access was established via webshells dropped on Centreon servers exposed to the internet.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Sandworm conducted a prolonged intrusion campaign from late 2017 to 2020 targeting Centreon monitoring systems, deploying webshells like P.A.S. and Exaramel for persistent access and likely utilizing existing C2 channels to exfiltrate data.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use established command and control (C2) channels to encode stolen data into normal communication protocols for exfiltration from Centreon systems compromised by Sandworm actors.

### Gaps Identified
- Lack of specific OCSF fields for network activity, process names, and DNS queries limits the precision in detecting C2 communications and encoded data exfiltration.
- Limited research into the specific techniques and IoCs used for exfiltrating data via webshells like P.A.S. and Exaramel leaves gaps in understanding how these tools facilitate data transfer.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use established command and control (C2) channels to encode stolen data into normal communication protocols for exfiltration from Centreon systems compromised by Sandworm actors.
- Gap: Lack of specific OCSF fields for network activity, process names, and DNS queries limits the precision in detecting C2 communications and encoded data exfiltration.
- Focus: Enhance detection capabilities to identify anomalies in regular C2 traffic that could indicate data encoding and exfiltration activities by Sandworm actors. Develop signatures or machine learning models to detect patterns indicative of data being embedded within normal protocol flows.
- Gap: Limited research into the specific techniques and IoCs used for exfiltrating data via webshells like P.A.S. and Exaramel leaves gaps in understanding how these tools facilitate data transfer.
- Focus: Investigate detailed telemetry from compromised Centreon systems to uncover unique communication patterns or payloads indicative of Sandworm's C2 and exfiltration activities. Collaborate with CERT-FR for IoCs and latest threat intelligence updates.
