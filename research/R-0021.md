## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Ransomware C2 servers act as communication hubs between attackers and compromised systems, allowing for the issuance of commands and data exfiltration. Communication with these servers often involves encrypted or obfuscated data transfers to evade detection.

### Key Findings
- Established bidirectional network connections from infected endpoints to external IP addresses indicative of C2 server activity.
- Detection of unusual outbound traffic patterns, such as periodic small data exchanges (e.g., 1,024 bytes) with potential XOR obfuscation in the message headers.
- Identification of encrypted or suspiciously formatted payloads being downloaded and executed on compromised systems following initial contact.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries use command and control (C2) techniques in ransomware operations to maintain covert communication with infected devices, deliver malicious instructions or payloads, steal data before encryption, and evade detection through methods like encryption, obfuscation, and dynamic DNS services. C2 channels are critical for coordinating attacks and ensuring the success of ransom demands.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries use various techniques to disrupt and take control over C2 systems, including using remote access tools such as PsExec.
- Ransomware groups rely heavily on C2 channels to coordinate attacks, steal credentials, and exfiltrate data before encryption.
- Dynamic DNS services are employed by attackers to mask the true destination of their communications and evade detection mechanisms.
- Encryption and obfuscation techniques help adversaries in maintaining covert communication with compromised systems.
