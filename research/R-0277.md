## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
North Korean threat actors, under the guise of Moonstone Sleet and employing sophisticated social engineering tactics, have launched a campaign targeting developers through fake coding interviews. These malicious actors exploit technical hiring processes by delivering malware during dependency installations or coding assessments to steal developer credentials.

### Key Findings
- Threat actors use fake job interview emails to target higher education organizations and developers.
- Malware is delivered through technical assessment platforms, exploiting the process of installing dependencies or conducting coding interviews.
- This campaign represents a sophisticated evolution in social engineering tactics, transforming hiring processes into an attack vector.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
North Korean actors are employing a sophisticated phishing campaign that involves posing as IT workers to conduct fake coding interviews, aiming to steal developer credentials. This tactic leverages malware such as BeaverTail and InvisibleFerret to compromise password stores and web browsers of unsuspecting victims.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries pose as legitimate IT recruiters or hiring managers to lure developers into rigged interview sessions.
- The campaign utilizes a video conferencing app called BeaverTail for credential harvesting.
- Malware like InvisibleFerret is deployed to steal credentials from password management tools and web browsers.
- North Korean attackers are expanding their phishing methods by targeting a broader range of job seekers beyond just developers.
