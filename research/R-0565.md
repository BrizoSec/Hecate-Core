## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Russian cyber espionage actors often leverage compromised systems within a target network to establish long-term persistence and conduct surveillance. They frequently use sophisticated malware and command-and-control (C2) infrastructure to exfiltrate data undetected, maintaining operational security by rotating C2 servers and employing domain fronting techniques.

### Key Findings
- Russian APT groups commonly deploy custom malware with remote access capabilities to maintain persistence within a compromised network.
- [UNCERTAIN] The use of legitimate commercial services like Microsoft Office 365 for command-and-control communications is a favored technique among Russian cyber espionage actors, enabling them to blend in with normal traffic and evade detection.
- Russian cyber operations often include the deployment of DGA (Domain Generation Algorithms) to create large numbers of potential C2 domains, complicating network defense efforts.
- Encryption and tunneling protocols are frequently employed by these groups to secure exfiltrated data during transit, reducing the likelihood of detection.
- Persistence mechanisms such as registry key modifications or scheduled tasks in Windows operating systems are commonly used to ensure malware continues to operate even after a system restart.
- Russian cyber espionage campaigns often involve spear-phishing emails with tailored content designed to trick high-value targets into executing malicious payloads.
