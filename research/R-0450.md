## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
OctLurk and SilkLurk are backdoors discovered by Kaspersky targeting government organizations in Central Asia. These sophisticated malware families use a combination of encryption techniques, such as double-XOR decryption and zlib decompression, to hide their presence and evade detection.

### Key Findings
- The backdoor uses victim-specific XOR keys derived from system paths for decryption purposes.
- Four distinct C2 (Command and Control) servers are utilized to communicate with compromised systems.
- A network traffic management tool called LurkProxy is used alongside OctLurk and SilkLurk to manipulate network communications.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are using the backdoors OctLurk and SilkLurk to target government organizations in Central Asia, leveraging them to access and manipulate LSASS memory for credential harvesting and lateral movement.

### Key Findings
- OctLurk and SilkLurk are deployed by a suspected Chinese-speaking threat actor targeting government entities in Central Asia since January 2025.
- The attackers use LurkProxy, a network traffic management tool, to manage the backdoors' operation and facilitate the download of additional malicious plugins.
- OctLurk employs double-XOR decryption with zlib decompression for its backdoor payload delivery, enhancing evasion techniques against detection.
- Victims include organizations across various sectors in Central Asia as well as some entities in Syria, indicating a potential expansion of targeted attacks.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Adversaries are using the sophisticated malware families OctLurk and SilkLurk to target government organizations in Central Asia, employing them for credential harvesting from LSASS memory and lateral movement. These backdoors utilize advanced evasion techniques such as double-XOR decryption with zlib decompression, making detection challenging.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use manipulation of LSASS process memory to harvest credentials and facilitate lateral movement on government entities in Central Asia.

### Gaps Identified
- Limited telemetry for file_event:monitored_file_access makes it difficult to detect access to specific API functions or registry keys that indicate LSASS memory manipulation activities.
- There is a significant underreporting of credential_access_attempt events, suggesting that many organizations lack the necessary monitoring tools and alerts for such suspicious activity.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use manipulation of LSASS process memory to harvest credentials and facilitate lateral movement on government entities in Central Asia.
- Gap: Limited telemetry for file_event:monitored_file_access makes it difficult to detect access to specific API functions or registry keys that indicate LSASS memory manipulation activities.
- Gap: There is a significant underreporting of credential_access_attempt events, suggesting that many organizations lack the necessary monitoring tools and alerts for such suspicious activity.
- Focus: Enhance detection capabilities by implementing more robust file_event:monitored_file_access telemetry to monitor access patterns indicative of LSASS memory manipulation.
- Focus: Increase coverage of credential_access_attempt events through advanced security tools and enhanced alerting mechanisms to promptly detect and respond to suspicious activities involving credentials.
