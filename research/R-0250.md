## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
TELEPUZ is a modular malware-as-a-service (MaaS) threat that uses encrypted data blobs to deliver its payload in memory, transitioning from executable files to more stealthy methods. It leverages smart contracts for dynamic and flexible payload delivery, enhancing operational security.

### Key Findings
- TELEPUZ employs encryption and obfuscation techniques to disguise malicious payloads as legitimate file types.
- The malware uses in-memory execution to avoid detection by traditional disk-based antivirus solutions.
- Smart contracts are utilized to dynamically deliver payloads, providing attackers with a flexible and resilient delivery mechanism.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries abuse the ClickFix social engineering technique by misleading users into believing they are applying quick fixes to common computer issues, which actually leads them to execute malicious payloads. This method often involves compromised websites and clipboard hijacking to spread malware such as TELEPUZ, an infostealer or RAT.

### Key Findings
- ClickFix leverages social engineering to trick users into executing malicious code through seemingly benign 'quick fixes'.
- The technique is employed in conjunction with other malware families like CLICKFIX-VIDAR chains to spread TELEPUZ, a modular MaaS (Malware as a Service) solution.
- Compromised websites are used as vectors for initial infection and distribution of the ClickFix payload.
- Clipboard hijacking plays a crucial role in this attack vector, allowing threat actors to inject malicious URLs or scripts into user interactions.
- TELEPUZ often deploys infostealers or RATs upon execution, targeting sensitive information and providing remote access capabilities to attackers.
- ClickFix is part of an evolving trend where adversaries continuously refine social engineering techniques to circumvent security measures.
