## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
ValleyRAT is a malware variant that spreads by masquerading as adware, using techniques like DLL sideloading and process hollowing to evade detection. It operates by downloading a malicious MSI package which disables Windows Defender and runs with elevated privileges to collect system information and execute additional payloads.

### Key Findings
- ValleyRAT begins its infection through phishing emails or malicious websites that trick users into downloading a seemingly legitimate MSI package, which actually installs the malware.
- The malware disguises itself by modifying a signed executable of QN Wallpaper, allowing it to bypass antivirus detection as users often add such trusted files to exclusions.
- ValleyRAT employs process hollowing and DLL sideloading techniques to inject malicious code into legitimate processes, ensuring stealthy execution and data collection.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
The threat actor Silver Fox is distributing the ValleyRAT backdoor disguised as a signed Chinese adware application. This method allows the malware to evade detection by tricking users into adding it to their antivirus exclusion lists.

### Key Findings
- ValleyRAT spreads through adware distribution, using DLL sideloading techniques to bypass security measures and gain full control over infected machines.
- The malware is disguised as legitimate software such as QN Wallpaper, performing decoy actions like installing popular applications to mask its malicious activities.
- Silver Fox leverages signed binaries to increase the credibility of their payloads, making it more likely that users will trust and install the malware without suspicion.
- ValleyRAT has been observed in various campaigns targeting primarily Chinese-speaking users through coordinated adware networks, indicating a strategic focus on this demographic.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
ValleyRAT is a sophisticated malware variant that spreads by masquerading as adware, primarily targeting Chinese-speaking users. It employs advanced techniques such as DLL sideloading and process hollowing to evade detection, while also leveraging signed binaries to increase its credibility.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use DLL sideloading and process hollowing to execute ValleyRAT on Windows endpoints disguised as adware with the goal of stealing sensitive information from targeted users.

### Gaps Identified
- Current antivirus solutions may not effectively detect modifications made to trusted binaries, such as QN Wallpaper, which allows ValleyRAT to evade detection by being added to exclusion lists.
- Limited visibility into outbound DNS and HTTP requests that do not match known malicious patterns, making it difficult to identify data exfiltration or command-and-control communications in real-time.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use DLL sideloading and process hollowing to execute ValleyRAT on Windows endpoints disguised as adware with the goal of stealing sensitive information from targeted users.
- Gap: Current antivirus solutions may not effectively detect modifications made to trusted binaries, such as QN Wallpaper, which allows ValleyRAT to evade detection by being added to exclusion lists.
- Gap: Limited visibility into outbound DNS and HTTP requests that do not match known malicious patterns, making it difficult to identify data exfiltration or command-and-control communications in real-time.
- Focus: Enhance monitoring for unexpected process creations of unfamiliar binaries, especially those originating from temporary directories or trusted application paths. This will help detect the initial execution of ValleyRAT after installation as adware.
- Focus: Increase scrutiny on DNS and HTTP traffic analysis to identify suspicious connections with known malicious domains or IP addresses associated with ValleyRAT's command-and-control infrastructure.
