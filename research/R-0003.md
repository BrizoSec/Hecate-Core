## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Aeternum is a sophisticated botnet loader that leverages the Polygon blockchain for its decentralized command-and-control (C2) infrastructure. The system stores encrypted commands in smart contracts, which are queried by infected devices through public RPC endpoints.

### Key Findings
- Infected devices query commands from smart contracts on the Polygon blockchain via public RPC endpoints.
- Smart contracts use storage slots to store administrative data such as the admin wallet address and an encrypted domain.
- Commands stored in the blockchain are immutable once confirmed, enhancing the resilience of Aeternum's C2 operations.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Aeternum is a C++ botnet loader that employs the Polygon blockchain for its command-and-control (C2) operations, using smart contracts and RPC queries to retrieve instructions. This approach makes it more resilient against takedown efforts by traditional methods.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries use the Polygon blockchain to host C2 infrastructure in a decentralized manner through smart contracts.
- Aeternum retrieves encrypted or plaintext instructions via RPC queries, enhancing operational security and resilience.
- The migration from self-hosted C2 mechanisms to blockchain-based systems indicates an evasion technique that complicates detection and mitigation efforts.
- Detection of Aeternum requires monitoring for activities related to the Polygon network, including smart contract interactions.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Aeternum is a sophisticated botnet loader that leverages the Polygon blockchain for its decentralized command-and-control (C2) infrastructure, storing encrypted commands in smart contracts and querying them through public RPC endpoints. This method enhances operational resilience against traditional takedown efforts.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use blockchain-based C2 infrastructure to host immutable, encrypted instructions that are queried by infected devices via RPC calls to evade detection and maintain persistent control over compromised systems.

### Gaps Identified
- The current telemetry coverage lacks comprehensive monitoring of Polygon network interactions and smart contract queries, which may hinder the ability to detect Aeternum's activities early on.
- There is insufficient knowledge about how adversaries might adapt their blockchain C2 methods beyond the Polygon platform to other blockchains with similar capabilities, posing a challenge for long-term threat mitigation strategies.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use blockchain-based C2 infrastructure to host immutable, encrypted instructions that are queried by infected devices via RPC calls to evade detection and maintain persistent control over compromised systems.
- Gap: The current telemetry coverage lacks comprehensive monitoring of Polygon network interactions and smart contract queries, which may hinder the ability to detect Aeternum's activities early on.
- Gap: There is insufficient knowledge about how adversaries might adapt their blockchain C2 methods beyond the Polygon platform to other blockchains with similar capabilities, posing a challenge for long-term threat mitigation strategies.
- Focus: Enhancing network traffic analysis tools to monitor and detect suspicious RPC queries directed at known or newly discovered blockchain-based C2 infrastructure on the Polygon network.
- Focus: Investigating and documenting additional blockchain platforms that could be used by adversaries for decentralized C2 operations, in order to broaden defensive capabilities against similar threats.
