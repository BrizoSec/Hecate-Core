## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries use the Cruciferra crypter service to evade detection by packing malware into encrypted payloads, which are then unpacked on the target system using T1014 (Rootkit) technique. This method helps in hiding malicious activities from security tools and analysts.

### Key Findings
- Cruciferra employs sophisticated encryption techniques to protect malicious payloads, complicating their detection by traditional antivirus solutions.
- Adversaries leverage rootkits to intercept system calls and hide the presence of malware after it has been unpacked on the target machine.
- The use of command-and-control (C2) infrastructure is observed for maintaining persistence and furthering the attack after initial compromise.
- CrowdStrike Falcon Adversary Intelligence provides valuable insights into identifying and responding to such threats by detecting anomalies associated with T1014.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
The analysis of Cruciferra, a sophisticated crypter service, reveals that adversaries utilize rootkits (T1014) to evade detection by encrypting and hiding malicious payloads on target systems. The research highlights the importance of monitoring system API hooks, process creation, registry changes, and network communications for detecting such evasion tactics.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use rootkit techniques (T1014) to evade detection by traditional security measures, allowing them to maintain persistence and control over compromised systems.

### Gaps Identified
- There is a lack of detailed analysis on how rootkits modify specific system APIs and the extent of their impact on system integrity and functionality.
- Limited understanding of the correlation between encrypted payloads delivered via Cruciferra and subsequent C2 infrastructure communications post-unpacking, which complicates timely threat detection.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use rootkit techniques (T1014) to evade detection by traditional security measures, allowing them to maintain persistence and control over compromised systems.
- Gap: There is a lack of detailed analysis on how rootkits modify specific system APIs and the extent of their impact on system integrity and functionality.
- Focus: Enhancing monitoring for anomalous process creation and registry modifications related to service entries and driver load points to detect early signs of rootkit activity.
- Gap: Limited understanding of the correlation between encrypted payloads delivered via Cruciferra and subsequent C2 infrastructure communications post-unpacking, which complicates timely threat detection.
- Focus: Developing advanced analytics within MISP for identifying patterns in network traffic that signify covert communication channels used by rootkits.
