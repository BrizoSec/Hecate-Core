## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Elastic InfoSec's agentic SOC architecture utilizes specialized agents that load skills on demand to handle frequent, routine tasks more efficiently. This approach reduces costs by 5× compared to general-purpose agents due to optimized token consumption and fewer large language model (LLM) calls.

### Key Findings
- Specialized agents reduce the cost of an agentic SOC by up to 5.7 times through efficient skill loading and execution.
- Skills in Elastic InfoSec load on demand, which minimizes unnecessary LLM interactions, thereby reducing costs.
- The quality of specialized agent performance improves as their capabilities grow without degrading existing functionalities.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries could potentially exploit the efficiency gains of inlined skills within specialized agents by targeting the narrow focus these agents possess, creating scenarios that bypass or overwhelm their specific expertise. This might involve crafting attacks that mimic legitimate activities to avoid detection by narrowly-focused alert triage.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries may use social engineering tactics to gather information on which agents are responsible for certain alerts and how they operate in order to tailor their attack vectors accordingly.
- Attackers could develop campaigns that exploit the reduced flexibility of specialized agents, such as crafting traffic patterns or malicious activities that fall outside the typical scope of these agents' responsibilities but still cause significant harm.
- Indicators of Compromise (IoCs) might include unusual activity in areas not covered by a specific agent's inlined skills or unexpected interactions with systems that the agent is not designed to monitor closely.
- Tools used by adversaries could be sophisticated malware or scripts designed to mimic legitimate network traffic, thereby evading detection by specialized agents optimized for different threat vectors.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Elastic InfoSec's agentic SOC leverages specialized agents with inlined skills to achieve up to a 5.7x cost reduction by minimizing unnecessary large language model interactions, but this efficiency could expose the system to targeted attacks exploiting the narrow focus of these agents.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use social engineering and tailored attack vectors to evade specialized SOC agents designed for specific threat scenarios, thereby bypassing detection mechanisms.

### Gaps Identified
- There is a lack of comprehensive monitoring and alerting for activities outside the typical scope of specialized agents' inlined skills.
- Current telemetry captures a high rate of user actions but may miss events not covered by specific agent responsibilities, leading to potential blind spots in detection.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use social engineering and tailored attack vectors to evade specialized SOC agents designed for specific threat scenarios, thereby bypassing detection mechanisms.
- Gap: There is a lack of comprehensive monitoring and alerting for activities outside the typical scope of specialized agents' inlined skills.
- Focus: Enhance threat intelligence sharing to stay informed about new adversarial tactics that target the limitations of specialized SOC agents.
- Gap: Current telemetry captures a high rate of user actions but may miss events not covered by specific agent responsibilities, leading to potential blind spots in detection.
- Focus: Expand the scope of anomaly detection to include patterns and activities outside typical agent coverage areas.
