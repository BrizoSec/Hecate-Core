## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
PLCs are embedded systems used to control industrial processes and machinery. In the context of critical infrastructure, they manage essential services like power grids, water treatment plants, and manufacturing facilities. The exploitation of PLCs by nation-state actors poses significant risks, including operational disruption and persistent access that can threaten national security.

### Key Findings
- PLC systems in U.S. critical infrastructure are being targeted for exploitation by sophisticated threat actors.
- Nation-state attackers maintain long-term access to these systems, indicating a high level of persistence and intent to cause sustained harm or disruption.
- Identity-driven intrusions represent a significant risk vector, emphasizing the importance of robust identity management and authentication mechanisms in protecting against such threats.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are exploiting compromised third-party infrastructure to deploy webshells for direct control over PLCs in critical U.S. infrastructure, and they use adversary-in-the-middle attacks to intercept network traffic for further exploitation.

### Key Findings
- Use of TwoFace webshell for direct interaction with compromised systems
- Employment of credential harvesters as part of phishing campaigns
- Execution of adversary-in-the-middle (AiTM) techniques to modify real-time network traffic
- Leveraging pre-attack infrastructure (T1334 PRE-ATT&CK technique) by compromising third-party entities for command and control
- Presence of post-exploitation tools on compromised systems for maintaining access and conducting further attacks
- Utilization of webshells to execute arbitrary commands and scripts directly on PLCs

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Nation-state actors are exploiting PLC systems within critical U.S. infrastructure, employing sophisticated tactics such as webshells and credential harvesting to gain persistent access and disrupt operations.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use automated cloud exploitation tools and compromised third-party infrastructure to deploy webshells for direct control over PLCs in order to persistently monitor and manipulate critical U.S. infrastructure systems.

### Gaps Identified
- There is a lack of detailed telemetry on file creation/modification events specific to ICS software, which could provide crucial insights into unauthorized changes made by adversaries.
- Current coverage does not adequately address the monitoring and detection of adversary-in-the-middle (AiTM) techniques used to intercept and manipulate real-time network traffic in industrial control systems.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use automated cloud exploitation tools and compromised third-party infrastructure to deploy webshells for direct control over PLCs in order to persistently monitor and manipulate critical U.S. infrastructure systems.
- Gap: There is a lack of detailed telemetry on file creation/modification events specific to ICS software, which could provide crucial insights into unauthorized changes made by adversaries.
- Gap: Current coverage does not adequately address the monitoring and detection of adversary-in-the-middle (AiTM) techniques used to intercept and manipulate real-time network traffic in industrial control systems.
- Focus: Strengthening identity management and authentication mechanisms within ICS environments to prevent identity-driven intrusions and protect against sophisticated nation-state threats.
- Focus: Enhancing network traffic analysis capabilities for protocols specific to industrial control systems (e.g., MODBUS, S7, DNP3) to detect anomalous patterns indicative of PLC exploitation.
