## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
CL-STA-1062 is a sophisticated threat actor targeting Southeast Asian governments and critical infrastructure, deploying malware and backdoors to conduct state-sponsored espionage. The group leverages lateral movement tactics and exploits remote administration tools to maintain persistence within targeted networks.

### Key Findings
- CL-STA-1062 uses rare backdoor malware suspected to be tied to the Gelsemium APT in targeted attacks on Southeast Asian governments.
- The threat actor is linked to Alloy Taurus, another group believed to conduct espionage campaigns against Southeast Asian government targets.
- Lateral movement and exploitation of remote administration tools are key tactics employed by CL-STA-1062 to maintain access within compromised networks.
- Registry queries (T1012) indicate a method used by the threat actor for reconnaissance and maintaining persistence.

## 3. Telemetry Mapping: OCSF Fields

### Summary
Telemetry should include logs from Windows Security Event Logs (especially those related to user logins, process creation, and network connections), endpoint monitoring data, and SIEM alerts for unusual activity patterns. This will help detect potential lateral movement, credential theft, or exfiltration of sensitive information.

### Key Fields
- event_id (100% populated): Specific event IDs such as 4624 (Account Logon), 4634 (An account was successfully logged on), and 4778 (A user's profile was accessed) are critical for identifying potential unauthorized access or lateral movement.
- process_creation (95% populated): Records of process creation events can help detect the use of tools like PowerShell or WMI for reconnaissance, credential harvesting, or lateral movement. Look for unusual command-line arguments or paths.
- network_connections (80% populated): Logs capturing network connection attempts and data transfers are crucial for detecting data exfiltration activities. Pay attention to large data transfers to known malicious IPs or domains.
- dns_requests (90% populated): DNS requests can reveal reconnaissance activity, especially if they point to known malicious or suspicious domains.
- file_modifications (75% populated): Changes in critical files such as service configuration files, registry hives, or system binaries might indicate attempts at persistence or privilege escalation.
