## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Dropcatch refers to the process where domain names that have expired and been deleted from their original registrar become available for registration by other registrars or individuals through auctions or first-come, first-served mechanisms. Scavengers in this context are entities that actively monitor and attempt to register these domains, often with the intention of monetizing them, especially if they were previously associated with malicious activities.

### Key Findings
- Expired domains can be repurposed by scavengers who register them for profit or other nefarious purposes after they have been dropped back into the domain name system (DNS).
- Scavenging activities often target expired domains that had high traffic, valuable branding, or were involved in cybercrime to maximize potential gains.
- The practice of dropcatching can inadvertently lead to the reactivation and amplification of malicious web content if scavengers do not perform proper due diligence on the history of a domain.


---

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries exploit dropcatch services by acquiring expired domains once owned or used for malicious purposes, such as phishing, malware distribution, and command-and-control (C2) infrastructure. They then repurpose these domains to conduct further cyberattacks without the need for establishing new fraudulent domain registrations.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries often use dropcatch services to obtain expired but previously compromised domains that have established trust among potential victims or have been indexed by search engines and web directories.
- [UNCERTAIN] Some adversaries may engage in a cycle of registering, using briefly for malicious activities, allowing the domain to expire, and then re-registering it through dropcatch services to evade detection mechanisms like blacklists.
- Commonly observed techniques include leveraging expired domains for phishing campaigns by mimicking legitimate websites or redirecting users to malware-laden sites.
- Indicators of compromise may include sudden spikes in traffic to recently registered but historically malicious domains, unusual patterns of DNS requests, and unexpected communications with IP addresses associated with known bad actors.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Dropcatch scavengers exploit expired domains that were previously used for malicious purposes, re-registering them to conduct further cyberattacks. This practice can lead to the resurgence of phishing campaigns and malware distribution without the need for establishing new fraudulent domain registrations.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use dropcatch services to re-acquire expired domains once used for malicious activities in order to evade detection mechanisms and conduct further cyberattacks on unsuspecting victims.

### Gaps Identified
- There is a lack of comprehensive due diligence checks by scavengers before registering these dropped domains, leading to potential security risks as the domains can be quickly repurposed for nefarious use.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use dropcatch services to re-acquire expired domains once used for malicious activities in order to evade detection mechanisms and conduct further cyberattacks on unsuspecting victims.
