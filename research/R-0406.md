## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Threat actors are using Microsoft Teams and Quick Assist to impersonate IT support, tricking victims into initiating a session that grants remote access. This initial foothold is often followed by the deployment of GoGRPC backdoor for persistent access, leading to credential theft and ransomware infections.

### Key Findings
- Attackers use vishing tactics to诱使受害者启动Quick Assist会话，从而获得远程访问权限。
- 一旦获取初始访问权，攻击者通常会使用GoGRPC后门进行持久化访问，并进一步部署恶意软件。
- 大多数此类事件集中在北美地区，美国受影响最为严重。

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are exploiting social engineering tactics through Microsoft Teams to impersonate helpdesk personnel, tricking victims into initiating Quick Assist sessions. Once access is gained, they deploy the GoGRPC backdoor, which communicates over gRPC and HTTP/2 to maintain persistence while evading detection.

### Key Findings
- Threat actors initiate social engineering attacks via Teams calls, masquerading as helpdesk staff to gain initial access through Quick Assist.
- GoGRPC backdoors are deployed post-compromise to establish a persistent foothold and facilitate lateral movement within the network.
- Attackers use gRPC over HTTP/2 for C2 communications, enabling stealthy data exfiltration and command execution.
- The deployment of GoGRPC indicates adversaries' shift towards more sophisticated communication protocols to bypass traditional security measures.
- Phishing campaigns precede social engineering attacks, overwhelming the target's inbox with unsolicited emails before initiating Teams calls.
- Detection challenges arise due to the legitimate nature of Quick Assist and Teams, making it difficult for defenders to distinguish malicious activity.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Threat actors are leveraging Microsoft Teams and Quick Assist to initiate unauthorized remote access sessions, typically followed by the deployment of GoGRPC backdoors for persistent network access. This attack vector involves sophisticated social engineering tactics, use of gRPC over HTTP/2 for C2 communications, and data exfiltration using local system sources.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use social engineering via Teams Quick Assist to gain initial access and subsequently deploy GoGRPC backdoor for persistent lateral movement and data exfiltration on corporate networks in the US and North America.

### Gaps Identified
- Current detection mechanisms may not effectively monitor gRPC over HTTP/2 communications, which is used by adversaries for stealthy command and control (C2) activities following initial access.
- Limited visibility into parent processes initiating suspicious activity can hinder understanding the full context of an attack, making it difficult to correlate initial compromise with subsequent malicious actions.

- Hypothesis: Adversaries use social engineering via Teams Quick Assist to gain initial access and subsequently deploy GoGRPC backdoor for persistent lateral movement and data exfiltration on corporate networks in the US and North America.
