## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries in cyber espionage often leverage compromised infrastructure to maintain persistent access and exfiltrate data over extended periods. They typically establish command-and-control (C2) servers or use existing malicious infrastructure such as botnets, ensuring their operations remain covert and resilient against detection and disruption.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries frequently employ domain fronting or proxy services to disguise C2 communications and evade network monitoring tools.
- [UNCERTAIN] Adversaries may establish a dedicated server farm in jurisdictions with lax cyber laws for hosting malicious infrastructure, enabling long-term espionage campaigns.
- Use of legitimate cloud service providers (CSPs) to host malware and distribute phishing kits allows adversaries to blend in with normal traffic patterns, reducing the likelihood of detection by security systems.
- Adversaries often implement multi-stage infection chains where initial access tools lead to secondary or tertiary infrastructure for data exfiltration, complicating incident response efforts.
- Lateral movement within a compromised network is facilitated through exploitation of trusted relationships between hosts and services, allowing adversaries to pivot across different segments of the victim's environment.
- Adversaries regularly update their C2 infrastructure with new domains or IP addresses to evade blacklisting and maintain operational security.
