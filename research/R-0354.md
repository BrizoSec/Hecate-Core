## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries may exploit the shift from static CVSS-based patching to dynamic risk assessment by identifying and targeting unpatched or less prioritized vulnerabilities that are not immediately addressed under a risk-based approach. They can also abuse the system by leveraging the latency between vulnerability discovery and mitigation, exploiting any gaps in real-time threat detection and response.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries may use sophisticated attack vectors to exploit newly identified but unpatched vulnerabilities before they are prioritized for remediation by the Falcon Platform's dynamic risk assessment model.
- Attackers can leverage lateral movement techniques within networks that have not yet patched critical assets, exploiting the latency between threat discovery and patch deployment.
- Indicators of Compromise (IOCs) associated with known threats may evolve into more sophisticated Indicators of Attack (IOAs), requiring continuous monitoring to detect emerging threats.
- The integration of third-party EDR tools like Microsoft Defender can be compromised if not properly secured, offering adversaries another entry point for lateral movement and data exfiltration.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
The CrowdStrike Falcon Platform supports U.S. government agencies in adhering to CISA BOD-26-04 through risk-based security updates and real-time behavioral detection, yet adversaries may exploit the latency between threat discovery and mitigation by targeting unpatched vulnerabilities.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use sophisticated attack vectors to target newly identified but unpatched vulnerabilities before they are prioritized for remediation by the Falcon Platform's dynamic risk assessment model.

### Gaps Identified
- There is a gap in current coverage regarding how adversaries can exploit latency between vulnerability discovery and mitigation, particularly when leveraging less prioritized vulnerabilities under a risk-based approach.
- The integration of third-party EDR tools like Microsoft Defender with the Falcon Platform may introduce additional security risks if not properly secured, potentially offering another entry point for adversaries.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use sophisticated attack vectors to target newly identified but unpatched vulnerabilities before they are prioritized for remediation by the Falcon Platform's dynamic risk assessment model.
- Gap: There is a gap in current coverage regarding how adversaries can exploit latency between vulnerability discovery and mitigation, particularly when leveraging less prioritized vulnerabilities under a risk-based approach.
- Focus: A recommended focus area is to enhance continuous monitoring capabilities to detect evolving Indicators of Attack (IOAs) beyond traditional Indicators of Compromise (IOCs).
- Gap: The integration of third-party EDR tools like Microsoft Defender with the Falcon Platform may introduce additional security risks if not properly secured, potentially offering another entry point for adversaries.
- Focus: Another focus area is to investigate how lateral movement techniques within networks can be detected and mitigated before critical assets are patched.
