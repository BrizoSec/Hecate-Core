## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries abuse the Pass-the-Hash (PtH) technique to gain unauthorized access and maintain persistence within a network without needing to provide plaintext passwords. They can use stolen NTLM or other hash values to authenticate to services and systems, bypassing traditional authentication mechanisms.

### Key Findings
- Pass-the-hash attacks are used by adversaries to escalate privileges and move laterally within the network.
- The attack leverages stolen NTLM hashes of user accounts to perform authentication without knowing their plaintext password.
- Indicators of a Pass-the-Hash (PtH) attack include unusual or unauthorized use of administrative credentials, unexpected service-to-service authentications, and anomalous logon events with high privilege levels.
- Tools commonly used for PtH attacks include Mimikatz, which can extract NTLM hashes from memory.
- The threat level is medium due to the moderate risk associated with successful credential theft and subsequent unauthorized access.
