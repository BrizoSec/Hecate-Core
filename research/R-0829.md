## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
FortiGate firewalls and SSL VPN gateways utilize SHA-256 for password storage and provide internet-facing administrative interfaces that serve as the primary attack surface. Threat actors exploit exposed systems by harvesting credentials through stuffing and spraying, then cracking hashes offline to gain persistent access.

### Key Findings
- The campaign targets legacy SHA-256 password storage mechanisms within Fortinet devices to enable offline credential cracking via distributed GPU clusters.
- Attackers establish persistence by creating new administrative accounts on compromised FortiGate or SSL VPN gateways after gaining initial access.
- Post-compromise activities include Active Directory enumeration and the deployment of ransomware payloads linked to INC/Lynx affiliates, functioning as an initial-access broker operation.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
The FortiBleed campaign exploits legacy SHA-256 password storage and reused credentials in exposed Fortinet FortiGate firewalls and SSL VPNs to harvest and crack authentication data at scale using distributed GPU clusters. Upon gaining access, threat actors establish persistence by creating new administrative accounts and conduct Active Directory enumeration, often functioning as an initial-access broker for ransomware affiliates like INC/Lynx and Payload.

### Key Findings

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
The FortiBleed campaign targets exposed Fortinet SSL VPN gateways by exploiting legacy SHA-256 password storage to facilitate offline GPU-based credential cracking, functioning as an initial-access broker for ransomware affiliates. Detection must focus on distinguishing legitimate administrative lockout events from adversary-driven account modifications and correlating external brute-force patterns with internal persistence mechanisms.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use distributed GPU clusters to crack legacy SHA-256 hashes harvested from exposed Fortinet SSL VPNs to establish persistent administrative access for ransomware deployment on internet-facing network infrastructure

### Gaps Identified
- Lack of telemetry correlating high-volume failed authentication attempts (credential stuffing/spraying) against SSL VPN endpoints with subsequent successful creation of new local admin accounts in internal directory services
- Absence of monitoring for specific IPv4 indicators of compromise (e.g., 103.27.186.156, 80.75.212.113) within SSL/TLS handshake logs and firewall traffic flows targeting port 443 on FortiGate devices

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use distributed GPU clusters to crack legacy SHA-256 hashes harvested from exposed Fortinet SSL VPNs to establish persistent administrative access for ransomware deployment on internet-facing network infrastructure
- Gap: Lack of telemetry correlating high-volume failed authentication attempts (credential stuffing/spraying) against SSL VPN endpoints with subsequent successful creation of new local admin accounts in internal directory services
- Gap: Absence of monitoring for specific IPv4 indicators of compromise (e.g., 103.27.186.156, 80.75.212.113) within SSL/TLS handshake logs and firewall traffic flows targeting port 443 on FortiGate devices
- Focus: Monitor for anomalous creation of new administrative user accounts or modification of existing user groups within 15 minutes of successful authentication from external, high-reputation-scoring IPs
- Focus: Analyze SSL VPN session logs for rapid succession of logins followed by immediate account deletions or lockouts to identify initial-access broker activity prior to ransomware payload execution
