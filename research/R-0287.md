## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
The WhatsApp account takeover scam operates by tricking users into voting for a fraudulent account, exploiting social trust and the popularity of online polls. This type of attack typically involves phishing emails or direct messages that appear to come from trusted contacts.

### Key Findings
- Phishing tactics are used to exploit user trust in social media platforms.
- Scammers use official-looking communications to trick users into taking actions like voting, which can lead to further exploits such as account takeover.
- Users should verify the authenticity of requests through direct contact with known friends or by checking official channels.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are using a WhatsApp account takeover scam that exploits user trust through social engineering, where victims are tricked into voting for a fraudulent friend. This attack leverages an adversary-in-the-middle technique to monitor and manipulate data exchanged between applications and external storage on Android devices.

### Key Findings
- The scam utilizes phishing tactics to deceive users into clicking malicious links or downloading malware through the guise of helping a friend, thus compromising their WhatsApp account.
- Adversaries employ an adversary-in-the-disk technique specific to Android devices to intercept data exchanged between applications and external storage, enabling them to steal credentials and take over accounts.
- Credential dumping is a prevalent method used by attackers in this scam to extract user credentials stored on compromised devices or networks.
- The attack typically involves monitoring and manipulating the communication flow on WhatsApp to redirect users to fake login pages or malicious websites designed to capture their login details.
