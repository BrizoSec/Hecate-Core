## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Microsoft Teams vishing attacks involve threat actors sending spoofed chat requests to users, often impersonating IT support or system administrators. These attackers exploit external communication settings in Microsoft Teams and use social engineering tactics to trick victims into granting remote access or installing malware.

### Key Findings
- Spoofed Microsoft Teams chat requests from imposters claiming to be IT support are a common initial contact point for vishing attacks.
- External access configurations of Microsoft Teams that allow external users to initiate chats and meetings should be reviewed and restricted where possible.
- Investigation into external Teams calls purportedly originating from IT staff is necessary to identify potential vishing attempts.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries abuse Microsoft Teams through vishing attacks by impersonating IT staff to gain victims' trust and coerce them into executing remote monitoring and management (RMM) tools or custom malware, often leading to persistent access on victim systems. These attacks are part of a broader trend where threat actors exploit trusted collaboration platforms for social engineering.

### Key Findings
- Vishing attacks begin with benign chats or calls that lead victims to execute RMM tools or custom malware via Microsoft Teams.
- Threat actors leverage the credibility and daily usage of Microsoft Teams as an entry point for deploying malicious payloads.
- Attackers transition from initial vishing engagement to technical execution phases, often establishing long-term access through persistent remote administration tools or Node.js-based malware loaders.
- Malware delivery methods include phishing emails in conjunction with Teams-based social engineering tactics.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Microsoft Teams vishing attacks involve adversaries impersonating IT staff to initiate social engineering campaigns via chat or calls, leading victims to execute RMM tools or custom malware for persistent access. The research highlights the need for enhanced detection of initial benign engagements and subsequent technical execution phases.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use Microsoft Teams vishing attacks to establish persistent access on victim systems by leveraging social engineering tactics and impersonating IT staff.

### Gaps Identified
- Lack of specific telemetry tracking initial benign engagement patterns in Microsoft Teams that precede malicious payload delivery, making it challenging to detect the transition from social engineering to technical exploitation.
- Limited visibility into remote monitoring and management (RMM) tool executions initiated via Microsoft Teams, which are often used by adversaries as a stepping stone for deploying persistent malware.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use Microsoft Teams vishing attacks to establish persistent access on victim systems by leveraging social engineering tactics and impersonating IT staff.
- Gap: Lack of specific telemetry tracking initial benign engagement patterns in Microsoft Teams that precede malicious payload delivery, making it challenging to detect the transition from social engineering to technical exploitation.
- Gap: Limited visibility into remote monitoring and management (RMM) tool executions initiated via Microsoft Teams, which are often used by adversaries as a stepping stone for deploying persistent malware.
- Focus: Enhance detection capabilities for anomalous chat activities in Microsoft Teams that mimic legitimate IT support communications but lead to the execution of RMM tools or suspicious file downloads.
- Focus: Develop signatures and machine learning models to identify patterns of technical exploitation following social engineering phases, such as unusual outbound network connections from victim systems after engaging with suspicious Teams invitations.
