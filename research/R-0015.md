## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Kimwolf v7 is an advanced Android botnet that leverages HTTP/2 for DDoS attacks, making its traffic appear as legitimate browser activity. It employs Ethereum Name Service (ENS) for command and control (C2), with fallback to a Tor hidden service if ENS fails.

### Key Findings
- Kimwolf v7 uses HTTP/2 to mask attack traffic as normal browsing behavior, complicating DDoS mitigation efforts.
- The botnet targets Android TV boxes and employs a tiered C2 infrastructure, including Ethereum Name Service (ENS) with fallback to Tor hidden services.
- All C2 communications are routed through a local proxy listening on `127.0.0[.]1:23075`.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Kimwolf v7 is an advanced version of the Kimwolf Android/IoT botnet that enhances its distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) capabilities and strengthens its command and control (C2) resilience. It primarily targets Android TV boxes and set-top boxes, complicating DDoS mitigation efforts with HTTP/2 flood attacks that mimic legitimate browser traffic.

### Key Findings
- Kimwolf v7 upgrades its DDoS attack capabilities to use HTTP/2 floods, making detection more difficult due to the resemblance of attack traffic to normal browsing activity.
- The botnet employs Chrome browser fingerprints in its DDoS traffic to evade traditional application-layer DDoS mitigation techniques.
- Target devices include Android TV boxes and set-top boxes, suggesting a focus on connected entertainment systems that may have less robust security measures compared to other IoT devices.
- Kimwolf v7 has removed exploitation code from the botnet’s core functionality, indicating reliance on external loaders for infection distribution.
