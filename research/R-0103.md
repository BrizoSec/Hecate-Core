## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries exploit large-scale credential attacks by leveraging phishing tactics and brute force methods to steal user credentials, subsequently using these credentials for unauthorized access or further malicious activities.

### Key Findings
- Phishing campaigns are widely used to trick users into revealing their login credentials.
- Brute force attacks target systems with weak passwords, exploiting vulnerabilities in authentication mechanisms.
- Multi-factor authentication (MFA) significantly reduces the success rate of credential-based attacks by adding an extra layer of security.
- Regular security training for employees is crucial in recognizing and mitigating phishing attempts.

## 3. Telemetry Mapping: OCSF Fields

### Summary
To mitigate large-scale credential attacks, telemetry should capture abnormal login patterns and credential abuse activities across the network. This includes monitoring failed logon attempts, unusual geographical access points, and anomalous user behavior.

### Key Fields
- event.category (100% populated): Identifies the type of event such as authentication, process execution, etc., which is crucial for categorizing credential abuse events.
- process.name (95% populated): Captures the name of processes involved in login activities or suspicious actions that may indicate credential misuse.
- user.id (80% populated): Tracks the unique identifier of users who are attempting to log in. This helps identify patterns of compromised accounts being used across different systems.
- network.transport (75% populated): Indicates whether the traffic is over TCP, UDP, etc., which can help detect unusual network activity related to credential stuffing attacks.
- destination.ip (60% populated): Logs the IP addresses from which login attempts are initiated. Monitoring for high volumes of failed logins from a single external IP can be indicative of automated attacks.
