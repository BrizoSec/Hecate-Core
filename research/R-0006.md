## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
AI-assisted development involves integrating AI systems into software engineering workflows to support tasks such as code review, testing, and debugging. This integration lowers the skill barrier for creating sophisticated attack infrastructure, making it easier for threat actors to develop and deploy persistent threats.

### Key Findings
- The use of AI in software development significantly reduces the technical expertise required to create advanced cyber tools.
- AI-assisted development enhances developer productivity by automating routine tasks and improving code quality through continuous feedback loops.
- Threat actors are leveraging AI capabilities to streamline their operations, making it easier to develop sophisticated attack vectors with less specialized knowledge.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are increasingly leveraging AI to automate various stages of cyberattacks, including reconnaissance and malware development. This enhances their operational efficiency and reduces the skill level required for launching sophisticated attacks.

### Key Findings
- AI is used by adversaries to accelerate and scale their attack orchestration and reconnaissance processes.
- AI-assisted tools are employed in automating tasks such as credential theft and data exfiltration, making these operations more efficient and harder to detect.
- APT groups integrate generative AI into their campaigns to enhance coordination and persistence, targeting critical infrastructure and the space sector.
- New malware variants like Slopoly are developed with AI assistance, linked to persistent ransomware operations by threat actors such as Hive0163.

## 3. Telemetry Mapping: OCSF Fields

### Summary
Telemetry capturing this behavior would focus on monitoring for signs of unauthorized access and activity indicative of persistent threat operations. This includes tracking the execution of scripts or binaries associated with administrative tools (such as PowerShell) from non-standard locations, unexpected network connections to external IP addresses, and anomalies in remote session logins.

### Key Fields
- process.name: PowerShell or other command-line interpreters used for scripting
- network.destination.ip_address: External IPs not part of the organization's usual communication pattern
- event.id (Windows Event IDs): For example, 4624 (Account Logon) and 4778 (User Account was managed via remote system)
- process.parent.name: Common parent processes that are often abused by attackers for lateral movement such as svchost.exe or services.exe
- user.account.name: Accounts with elevated privileges used at unusual times or from non-standard locations

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
AI-assisted development is revolutionizing software engineering by reducing the technical expertise required for sophisticated cyberattacks. This trend enables threat actors to develop and deploy persistent threats more efficiently, targeting critical infrastructure with enhanced coordination and stealth.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use AI-assisted tools to accelerate reconnaissance and automate attack orchestration to enhance operational efficiency and reduce the skill level required for launching sophisticated attacks on critical infrastructure

### Gaps Identified
- There is a lack of existing research and hunting efforts specifically targeting AI-assisted persistent threat operations, indicating an emerging threat vector that needs immediate attention.
- Current telemetry lacks comprehensive detection mechanisms for identifying unauthorized access through non-standard locations or unexpected network connections indicative of AI-assisted attack vectors.
