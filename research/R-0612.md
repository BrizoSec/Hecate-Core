## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Recent university breaches have exposed vulnerabilities in identity management and the need for robust zero trust security frameworks. Universities are increasingly adopting multi-factor authentication to enhance security but still face challenges such as ransomware attacks, necessitating better incident response planning and data encryption practices.

### Key Findings

- Identity management vulnerabilities are a common factor in these breaches, highlighting the need for enhanced security measures such as zero trust architecture.
- Multi-factor authentication is increasingly being adopted to strengthen security, yet universities still face substantial risks requiring comprehensive incident response plans.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are exploiting the rich trove of sensitive data held by universities, including personal identifiers and financial records, through evolving attack methods such as ransomware and phishing campaigns that bypass multi-factor authentication (MFA). These attacks lead to significant financial losses and breaches of confidential information.

### Key Findings
- Universities are targeted for their extensive collection of valuable sensitive data, making them lucrative targets for cyber adversaries.
- Recent breaches include sophisticated phishing campaigns targeting multiple universities over several months, aimed at stealing account details even when multi-factor authentication is in place.
- Ransomware attacks on universities result in significant financial losses and the need to pay large sums to regain access to encrypted data.
- Historical breaches often go undetected for extended periods, highlighting delayed discovery issues that exacerbate the impact of security incidents.
