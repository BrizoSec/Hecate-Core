## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries like Mirage Kitten employ obfuscation techniques (T1027) to make their malware payloads harder to detect and analyze by encrypting, encoding, or otherwise obfuscating files. This is often done in conjunction with other evasion methods such as DNS tunneling to ensure persistent and stealthy communication.

### Key Findings
- Mirage Kitten uses the NightLedger backdoor and two tunneling tools that leverage file obfuscation (T1027) to evade detection and establish a covert command-and-control channel.
- The malware employs Dynamic API Resolution (T1027.007) within its code, resolving API functions dynamically to obscure its behavior from analysis tools.
- Mirage Kitten's campaign targets the Middle East and Africa regions, using Kerberoasting and DNS tunneling as part of their attack strategy for persistence and data exfiltration.
- The adversaries use legitimate file names related to well-known software vendors like HP, Intel, Adobe to blend in with normal system files and avoid detection.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Mirage Kitten, an APT group targeting the Middle East and Africa, employs sophisticated malware with obfuscation techniques to evade detection. The threat actor uses NightLedger backdoor and DNS tunneling alongside file obfuscation for persistent surveillance.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use obfuscated files or information (T1027) to evade detection and establish covert command-and-control channels on government, military, and private sector entities in the Middle East and Africa region.
