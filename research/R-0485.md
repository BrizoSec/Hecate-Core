## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
CaptiveCrunch is a sophisticated cyberespionage campaign where attackers manipulate hotel Wi-Fi captive portals to redirect users to fake login pages designed to steal Microsoft 365 credentials, including tokens that persist even after password resets.

### Key Findings
- Attackers use DNS and HTTP manipulation techniques to redirect victims to malicious websites mimicking official login interfaces.
- CaptiveCrunch is carried out by Storm-2945, a subgroup of the Russian state-backed Midnight Blizzard actor group.
- Once compromised, hotel Wi-Fi portals are used to deliver malware or steal credentials through fake Microsoft 365 sign-in pages.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Midnight Blizzard's CaptiveCrunch campaign exploits hotel Wi-Fi captive portals to redirect users to phishing pages designed to steal Microsoft 365 credentials, utilizing DNS manipulation and malware deployment. This attack is linked to the Russian state-backed APT29 group, specifically its Storm-2945 subgroup.

### Key Findings
- Attackers compromise hotel Wi-Fi captive portals to redirect users to malicious phishing sites designed for credential theft.
- The campaign targets Microsoft 365 users and employs DNS manipulation techniques to hijack user traffic.
- Once redirected, victims are exposed to fake login pages mimicking legitimate Microsoft services to steal credentials.
- Malware such as ChocoShell is deployed to target browser cookies, saved passwords, SSO tokens, and Wi-Fi credentials stored on the infected device.
