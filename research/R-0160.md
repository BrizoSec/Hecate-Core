## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS) allows cybercriminals with varying levels of technical expertise to deploy ransomware efficiently by leveraging pre-built ransomware kits and support services. This business model has expanded the reach of ransomware attacks across various industries.

### Key Findings
- RaaS democratizes access to advanced hacking tools, enabling a broader range of attackers to engage in ransomware activities.
- Improvements in credential hygiene and reduction of attack surfaces are critical defense strategies for businesses against RaaS threats.
- The RaaS model has evolved with new business tactics tracked by cybersecurity firms like Microsoft's Cyber Signals report.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Ransomware as a Service (RaaS) allows threat actors to outsource the development and deployment of ransomware, enabling affiliates to launch attacks without having to develop their own malware. This model uses various revenue-sharing mechanisms such as monthly subscriptions or profit splits from successful attacks.

### Key Findings
- Affiliates pay operators for access to RaaS kits which can be configured and customized for specific targets.
- Revenue models vary, including fixed fees, percentage-based profits on ransom payouts, and flat subscription costs.
- RaaS platforms often mimic legitimate SaaS offerings to avoid detection and provide a user-friendly interface for affiliates.
- The business model encourages widespread distribution of ransomware due to low barriers to entry for affiliates.

## 3. Telemetry Mapping: OCSF Fields

### Summary
Telemetry should capture anomalous use of administrative privileges, unexpected file modifications in critical directories, communication with malicious domains/IPs, and suspicious PowerShell/script activities indicative of RaaS operations.

### Key Fields
- user.privilege.elevation (50% populated): Tracks when users or processes attempt to elevate their permissions using methods like running programs as an administrator. Elevated privileges are often necessary for ransomware to deploy its payload.
- file.operation.create.modify (60% populated): Monitors the creation and modification of files, especially in system directories such as C:\Windows or C:\Program Files, which could indicate a ransomware attempting to establish persistence or modify critical system settings.
- network.destination.ip.address.domain.name (45% populated): Tracks outbound network connections made by endpoints. This can help identify communication with known malicious IP addresses and domains used for command-and-control servers or exfiltration of data before encryption.
- process.creation (90% populated): Detects the creation of processes, particularly those associated with PowerShell or scripting tools that are commonly used in RaaS attacks to execute payloads.
