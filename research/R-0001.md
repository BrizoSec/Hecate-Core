## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Attackers in Korea have been observed using Radmin and UltraVNC for remote control after initial intrusion, turning compromised systems into proxy servers and VPN nodes. They utilize PowerShell scripts and other tools like Netch-gateway to achieve their goals.

### Key Findings
- Radmin and UltraVNC are installed on compromised systems as part of a multi-stage attack to establish persistent control.
- Attackers employ PowerShell-based installers and watchdog tasks/services for deploying UltraVNC after gaining initial access via Radmin.
- Windows Defender preferences may undergo suspicious changes during these attacks, indicating attempts to evade detection.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries targeting South Korea have exploited legitimate remote access tools such as Radmin and UltraVNC to gain control of compromised systems, often installing additional software like Netch and CCProxy to create malicious proxy nodes. These actions are part of broader intrusion tactics aimed at acquiring infrastructure for persistent access and covert communication.

### Key Findings
- Attackers use Radmin and UltraVNC to remotely control compromised South Korean systems.
- Additional tools such as Netch, CCProxy, and SoftEther VPN are installed on infected machines to establish proxy nodes and maintain persistent access.
- Multiple threat actor groups including Lazarus Group have been involved in these attacks.
- The main goal of the attackers is to acquire infrastructure that can be used for covert communication and persistence.

These attacks often involve the installation of additional software such as Netch, CCProxy, and SoftEther VPN to create malicious proxy nodes for covert communication.

### Recommended Hypothesis

### Gaps Identified

- Current coverage does not account for changes made to Windows Defender preferences or other security settings that may indicate evasion attempts by attackers.
