## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Cybersecurity budget planning involves allocating a portion of an organization's IT spend to protect against cyber threats. Typically, this allocation ranges from 4% to 20%, depending on the size and needs of the company, with larger organizations tending to spend more.

### Key Findings
- Cybersecurity budgets usually account for 10-20% of IT expenditures in most companies.
- Smaller organizations typically allocate a smaller percentage (4%-10%) compared to larger ones.
- It is important to prioritize skilled staff and avoid diverting security funds to other purposes.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries aim to exploit gaps in cybersecurity measures by targeting environments that lack comprehensive security frameworks, focusing on areas where risk reduction and visibility are not prioritized. They seek easy entry points and leverage advanced techniques such as APTs to maintain long-term access.

### Key Findings
- Threat actors focus on exploiting weak points in system hardening to gain unauthorized access.
- Small businesses should allocate between 4% to 10% of their IT budget towards cybersecurity, emphasizing risk reduction and minimizing dwell time.
- Medium-sized firms are advised to spend between 8-15% on security measures, with a focus on balancing technology investments and personnel costs.
- Large enterprises need to invest more heavily (10-20%) in cybersecurity to protect against APTs and other sophisticated threats.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Cybersecurity budget planning is critical for organizations of all sizes, with budgets ranging from 4% to 20% of IT expenditures. Smaller businesses should allocate at least 4%-10%, while large enterprises need to invest more heavily to counteract sophisticated threats like APTs.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use targeted attacks to exploit weak points in system hardening and lack of comprehensive security frameworks to gain unauthorized access on small and medium-sized businesses with inadequate cybersecurity budgets.

### Gaps Identified
- There is a lack of specific guidance on how to allocate cybersecurity funds effectively for different organizational sizes and threat levels, leading to potential misallocation or underinvestment in critical areas.
- The research does not provide detailed insights into the impact of budget allocation on reducing dwell time and minimizing risk exposure, making it challenging to measure the effectiveness of security investments.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use targeted attacks to exploit weak points in system hardening and lack of comprehensive security frameworks to gain unauthorized access on small and medium-sized businesses with inadequate cybersecurity budgets.
- Gap: There is a lack of specific guidance on how to allocate cybersecurity funds effectively for different organizational sizes and threat levels, leading to potential misallocation or underinvestment in critical areas.
- Gap: The research does not provide detailed insights into the impact of budget allocation on reducing dwell time and minimizing risk exposure, making it challenging to measure the effectiveness of security investments.
- Focus: Organizations should prioritize skilled staff and technology investments that enhance visibility and reduce risk exposure, particularly for smaller businesses where cybersecurity budgets are limited.
- Focus: Further research is needed to quantify how different levels of budget allocation affect an organization's ability to detect and respond to advanced threats effectively.
