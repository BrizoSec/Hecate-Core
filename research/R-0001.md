## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Organizations typically use multi-layered security measures to protect against executive impersonation and invoice fraud. These include email authentication technologies like SPF, DKIM, and DMARC, alongside employee training programs focused on recognizing phishing attempts.

### Key Findings
- Email authentication protocols such as SPF, DKIM, and DMARC are crucial for verifying the legitimacy of emails purportedly from executives or vendors.
- Employee education plays a significant role in identifying suspicious activities that could indicate executive impersonation or invoice fraud.
- Organizations may implement additional controls like multi-factor authentication (MFA) for financial transactions to add an extra layer of security.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are leveraging AI technologies to impersonate executives within organizations through sophisticated communication and social engineering tactics. This allows them to initiate invoice fraud by manipulating staff into making unauthorized financial transactions, often bypassing traditional security measures due to the credibility of the executive persona.

### Key Findings
- AI-powered tools enable adversaries to create highly convincing communications that mimic an organization's executives, facilitating spear-phishing attacks.
- [UNCERTAIN] Specific AI models used by threat actors are not named or detailed in this context, but they likely include text generation and voice synthesis capabilities.
- Attackers target employees who handle financial transactions, such as accounting staff, with urgent requests to make payments to fraudulent vendors or change payment details.
- Indicators of compromise may include unusual email threads initiated by executives outside their normal communication patterns or unexpected changes in vendor information through unverified channels.
- [UNCERTAIN] The use of deepfake technology for voice impersonation might be employed to further convince victims over the phone or in video calls, though exact methodologies are not specified.
