## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Password spray attacks involve attackers attempting a few common passwords against multiple user accounts to gain unauthorized access. These attacks are stealthy and often successful because they rely on the prevalence of weak or reused passwords.

### Key Findings
- Password spray attacks account for more than one-third of all account compromises in organizations.
- Attackers use commonly known or easily guessed passwords against a large number of accounts, increasing their chances of success with at least one user having a weak password.
- Detection and prevention of such attacks require monitoring login attempts for unusually high volumes of failed logins using the same few passwords.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are employing password spraying techniques as part of the LSHIY campaign to gain unauthorized access to systems by attempting commonly used passwords across multiple accounts, thereby minimizing their risk of triggering account lockouts and detection mechanisms.

### Key Findings
- Password spraying is being utilized in an ongoing attack attributed to LSHIY, targeting numerous accounts with a set of common passwords to evade security measures that would otherwise detect brute force attempts.
- The MITRE ATT&CK framework categorizes this specific password spraying technique as T1110.003 under the Brute Force tactic and lists Agrius and APT28 as examples of adversaries known to employ similar methods.
- Adversaries are leveraging credential access techniques such as T1555.002, which involves extracting passwords from security databases on compromised systems, enhancing their ability to perform password spraying attacks effectively.
- While not directly related to the LSHIY attack, other forms of lateral movement and persistence involve Adversary-in-the-Middle (AiTM) techniques like T0830, allowing adversaries to manipulate network communications for further exploitation.

## 3. Telemetry Mapping: OCSF Fields

### Summary
Telemetry capturing a password spray attack involves tracking high volumes of failed login attempts across multiple user accounts from the same IP address. This includes network traffic data and authentication logs.

### Key Fields
- auth.failed_attempts (50% populated): Tracks unsuccessful authentication events, which are crucial for identifying patterns indicative of a password spray attack.
- network.remote_address (90% populated): Identifies the IP addresses from where login attempts originate, aiding in source attribution and threat actor identification.
- auth.user_id (85% populated): Monitors user accounts targeted by failed login attempts to understand which users are being systematically attacked.
