## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
APT34, a suspected Iranian threat group, targeted government entities in the Middle East by exploiting CVE-2017-11882 through macro-enabled XLS files delivered via phishing emails. The attackers used PowerShell backdoors to maintain persistence and lateral movement within the network.

### Key Findings
- APT34 exploited a Microsoft Office vulnerability (CVE-2017-11882) for initial access
- Phishing campaigns were conducted with macro-enabled XLS files as payload vectors
- PowerShell scripts were used to establish and maintain persistence within compromised networks

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
The Iranian threat group Black Shadow has carried out a destructive cyber campaign against government entities in the Middle East, including the U.S. and Israel, using tactics that combine espionage with psychological operations to disrupt IT systems and recovery mechanisms.

### Key Findings
- The attack targets both primary IT infrastructure and backup/recovery systems, aiming for maximum disruption and data loss.
- Black Shadow employs destructive malware to wipe hard drives and compromise critical system files, preventing normal business operations.
- Espionage activities are conducted alongside disruptive actions, suggesting a dual-purpose campaign that gathers intelligence while causing chaos.
- The use of psychological warfare techniques indicates an attempt to create panic and fear among the target entities' personnel.
