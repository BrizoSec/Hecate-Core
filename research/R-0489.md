They frequently employ domain generation algorithms (DGAs) for C2 communications to evade detection and maintain operational security.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries are increasingly using polymorphic malware that can alter its code signature to bypass traditional antivirus solutions.
- [UNCERTAIN] There is evidence suggesting the use of cloud infrastructure services for hosting malicious payloads, taking advantage of legitimate service providers' anonymity.
- Phishing campaigns have evolved to include more sophisticated social engineering techniques, often leveraging current events or personal information obtained through reconnaissance activities.
- Malware distribution via compromised websites and spam emails continues to be a primary method for initial compromise, with an observed increase in multi-stage infection chains.
- C2 communications are being conducted over common protocols like HTTP/HTTPS, but also increasingly using encrypted channels such as DNS exfiltration or ICMP tunneling to avoid detection by network security measures.
