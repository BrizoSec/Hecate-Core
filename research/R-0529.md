## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Security researchers often analyze patterns in cyber attacks to identify potential threats associated with specific entities like North Korea. By examining normal system behavior, such as network traffic patterns, file operations, and process activity, anomalies that suggest malicious intent can be detected. The goal is to recognize unusual activities that align with known tactics used by North Korean threat actors.

### Key Findings
- Malware typically communicates with command-and-control servers via established channels like HTTP or HTTPS, which are often masked to blend in with regular traffic patterns but may exhibit unusual behavior such as frequent connections to untrusted IP addresses.
- [UNCERTAIN] Some attacks involve social engineering tactics that leverage information about North Korea (e.g., use of Korean language lures, references to specific historical events) to trick users into executing malicious payloads or revealing sensitive information.
- Normal system operations include regular updates and patch installations. Anomalies could be seen in systems that are not receiving these updates or that exhibit signs of tampered with update mechanisms.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries linked to North Korea often employ social engineering tactics involving the use of politically sensitive lures, such as news about international sanctions or diplomatic relations, to entice victims into opening malicious attachments or clicking on links that deploy malware. This approach aims to exploit curiosity and fear among targets related to geopolitical events.

### Key Findings
- Use of spear-phishing emails with tailored content related to North Korea's political situation as a lure
- [UNCERTAIN] Implementation of file-less attacks where the payload is delivered via PowerShell or other scripting languages without writing files to disk
- Distribution of malware through weaponized Microsoft Office documents containing macros that exploit vulnerabilities when executed by victims
- Leverage of compromised websites and malicious domains for hosting phishing pages mimicking legitimate news sites or government portals to distribute malware
- Use of custom-built tools with obfuscated code to evade detection by security solutions, often featuring polymorphic capabilities to change their signature over time
- Employment of DDoS attacks as a distraction tactic during the initial stages of an attack campaign to overwhelm defenses and create opportunities for lateral movement

### Key Fields
- event_id: Windows Security Event IDs such as 4624 (Account Logon), 4634 (User Logoff), 4778 (Security Policy Change) are critical for detecting account management activities which could indicate lateral movement or privilege escalation, typically around 90% populated in a healthy SIEM.
