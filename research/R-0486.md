## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries often abuse legitimate system utilities and network protocols to maintain persistence and exfiltrate data undetected. They frequently leverage common tools in unexpected ways to evade detection by security systems.

### Key Findings
- Use of PowerShell for command execution and lateral movement within a network, as it provides extensive capabilities while leaving minimal traces in logs compared to other scripting languages.
- [UNCERTAIN] Potential use of DNS tunneling to exfiltrate data through seemingly benign DNS requests, allowing adversaries to bypass traditional network monitoring tools that may not scrutinize DNS traffic closely.
- Employment of living-off-the-land binaries (LotLB) such as PsExec and WMI to maintain persistence and execute commands remotely without downloading additional malware onto the target system.
- Adversaries increasingly rely on encrypted communication channels, often leveraging SSL/TLS protocols for Command and Control (C2) communications to avoid detection by network monitoring tools.
- The use of steganography within images or other files to hide C2 traffic or exfiltrate data without alerting security teams through anomaly-based detection systems.
- Deployment of fileless malware techniques, which reside solely in memory, making them difficult to detect with traditional signature-based antivirus solutions.
