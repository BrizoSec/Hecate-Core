## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries in the Phantom Stealer campaign abuse trusted business workflows to gain unauthorized access, using social engineering techniques to infiltrate systems. They employ PowerShell scripts and SMTP for data exfiltration after collecting sensitive information from local systems.

### Key Findings
- Phantom Stealer uses social engineering tactics to bypass security measures and gain initial access.
- Adversaries leverage PowerShell and SMTP protocols for the collection and exfiltration of data.
- The campaign targets cloud collaboration services, exploiting them as a vector for trusted workflows abuse.
- Detection is improved by monitoring scripts executing local file system interactions and identifying unusual SMTP traffic patterns.
- Memory forensics can reveal injected code or malicious processes indicative of data theft activities.
- Continuous monitoring for anomalies in the use of trusted business applications can help preempt such attacks.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Phantom Stealer is a sophisticated malware campaign that exploits trusted business workflows and leverages social engineering techniques to gain initial access. Once inside, it uses PowerShell scripts for data collection and SMTP with STARTTLS encryption for exfiltration, targeting cloud collaboration services.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use multi-stage tactics leveraging trusted business processes and PowerShell scripts to collect sensitive information from local systems with the goal of persistently exfiltrating data via SMTP.

### Gaps Identified
- Current monitoring lacks deep visibility into the initial social engineering stages that grant adversaries access, making early detection challenging.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use multi-stage tactics leveraging trusted business processes and PowerShell scripts to collect sensitive information from local systems with the goal of persistently exfiltrating data via SMTP.
- Gap: Current monitoring lacks deep visibility into the initial social engineering stages that grant adversaries access, making early detection challenging.
- Focus: Improve detection capabilities by monitoring for unusual patterns in PowerShell script execution and identifying deviations in SMTP traffic indicative of data exfiltration attempts.
