## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
A spambot is an automated system designed to send large volumes of unsolicited emails (spam) through compromised email servers. These bots often target users for phishing or to distribute malware by exploiting vulnerabilities in email systems, commonly using SMTP protocols.

### Key Findings
- Spambots typically use compromised accounts or networks to send out mass emails.
- They employ techniques such as IP spoofing and domain spoofing to evade detection.
- The content of spam messages often includes links to malicious sites or attachments that contain malware.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are employing an updated version of the Astaroth spambot to distribute malicious payloads via mass email campaigns. This technique leverages social engineering and exploits human vulnerabilities to trick recipients into executing malware.

### Key Findings
- The spambot is capable of large-scale, automated distribution of phishing emails that contain links or attachments leading to malicious content.
- [UNCERTAIN] The use of compromised legitimate email accounts may be employed to increase the credibility and success rate of these campaigns.
- Emails often mimic trusted organizations or individuals to encourage recipients to open suspicious attachments or click on harmful URLs.
- The malware payload can include banking trojans, designed to steal financial credentials from infected systems.
- [UNCERTAIN] Adversaries may integrate additional modules or plugins into Astaroth to evade detection and enhance its capabilities over time.

## 3. Telemetry Mapping: OCSF Fields

### Summary

### Key Fields

- file_change_event (75% populated): Monitors file system changes, including creation and deletion of files. This helps in identifying dropped malware files or other artifacts created during an infection.
