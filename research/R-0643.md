## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
The provided research context does not identify 'vibes' as a specific technical adversary technique, but rather uses the term metaphorically to argue that Security Operations Centers (SOCs) cannot rely on intuition or informal methods. Instead, defenders must adopt advanced tools and methodologies to keep pace with sophisticated, AI-driven attacks that traditional defenses often fail to detect.

### Key Findings
- SOCs risk overlooking subtle signs of compromise if they lack deep insight into adversary behavior and fail to utilize frameworks like MITRE ATT&CK for emulation and detection.
- Adversaries are increasingly leveraging AI capabilities, enabling single actors to hack multiple organizations rapidly, which necessitates that defenders also use advanced AI tools to maintain parity.
- Sophisticated attackers employ tactics such as hibernating worms/malware prior to execution, scanning networks extremely slowly, and using distractions to divert SOC attention from actual compromises.
- Traditional defense mechanisms are insufficient against sophisticated AI-driven attacks, requiring SOCs to balance operational control with ongoing maintenance needs by evaluating the buy, build-with, and DIY spectrum for AI solutions.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
The research highlights that reliance on intuitive or unverified AI-driven security operations creates critical blind spots where adversaries exploit trust in automated environments and the provenance of code/configurations. Effective hunting requires shifting from 'vibes' to verifiable telemetry and explainable reasoning to detect sophisticated, AI-augmented attacks that bypass traditional controls through slow, stealthy execution.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use hibernated malware payloads and extremely slow network scanning techniques to evade detection in SOC environments that rely on unverified trust models rather than explicit telemetry verification.

### Gaps Identified
- Lack of provenance verification for automated code/configurations allows attackers to inject malicious artifacts into trusted development or deployment pipelines without triggering standard integrity checks.
- Absence of explainable AI reasoning in SOC platforms prevents analysts from validating the source of alerts, leading to missed detections of subtle compromise indicators that appear as false positives or benign operational noise.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use hibernated malware payloads and extremely slow network scanning techniques to evade detection in SOC environments that rely on unverified trust models rather than explicit telemetry verification.
