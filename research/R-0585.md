## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries may abuse the AI-driven triage system by submitting misleading or crafted HackerOne reports to exploit the $2 cost per report and potentially evade detection. They could flood the system with false positives, causing delays in genuine threat identification and response, thereby exploiting the remaining 15% discrepancy between AI and human decisions.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries can submit numerous fabricated bug bounty reports to create noise and distract from real security issues, leveraging the low cost of $2 per report for automated triage.
- The multi-stage pipeline used in the AI system may be targeted by attackers who understand its classification stages to craft reports that bypass initial filters.
- SOC analysts reviewing host isolation decisions based on AI recommendations might face challenges due to an influx of false positives, leading to alert fatigue and reduced effectiveness.
- Attackers could exploit the 15% gap between AI and human accuracy in triage by targeting vulnerabilities or crafting exploits that fall into this margin of error.
