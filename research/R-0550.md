**Topic:** Inside Astaroth's New Spambot Component

## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
A spambot is typically part of malware designed to send large volumes of unsolicited emails often containing malicious links or attachments. It operates by harvesting email addresses from various sources and distributing spam across them, sometimes in coordination with other infected machines.

### Key Findings
- Spam messages are often distributed using compromised servers.
- The spambot frequently employs techniques to avoid detection such as changing IP addresses or using legitimate SMTP services.
- Harvesting of email addresses can be done through web scraping or by scanning local networks.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are leveraging Astaroth's spambot component to distribute malware via email campaigns, often employing social engineering tactics and exploiting user trust to maximize infection rates. This component is designed to be modular, allowing for easy integration of new techniques or payloads as threat intelligence improves.

### Key Findings
- Email-based phishing attacks are a primary vector for spreading Astaroth malware.
- [UNCERTAIN] The spambot may use compromised email accounts within organizations to send emails that appear legitimate and trustworthy to recipients, enhancing the success rate of the campaign.
- Attachments or links in these emails often lead to the download of additional malicious payloads from remote servers.
- Astaroth's spambot utilizes command-and-control (C2) infrastructure to receive instructions for new campaigns and update malware components.
- The use of obfuscated URLs and domain generation algorithms (DGAs) helps evade detection by security solutions.
- Social engineering techniques are employed in the crafting of email content, aiming to entice users into clicking on malicious links or downloading attachments.
