## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries abuse the pass-the-hash (PtH) technique by leveraging stolen hashed credentials to gain unauthorized access and maintain persistence within a network without needing the original passwords, thereby avoiding detection mechanisms that rely on credential compromise.

### Key Findings
- Stolen hashed credentials are used for PtH attacks, allowing adversaries to escalate privileges and move laterally through the network.
- Krawczyk Industries Limited reported a medium-level threat event on 2026-07-24, indicating that such attacks pose significant risks even in the future.
- The use of PtH techniques introduces challenges for traditional multi-factor authentication (MFA) systems as adversaries can bypass them by reusing hashes.
- Adversaries often target AI and machine learning systems embedded within modern enterprise infrastructures to enhance their attack vectors.
- Monitoring IOAs (Indicator of Attack) rather than just IOCs (Indicator of Compromise) can provide early detection and prevent PtH attacks from succeeding.
