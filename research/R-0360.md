## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries may exploit the dynamic risk-based vulnerability management system of CrowdStrike Falcon Platform by focusing on assets with high business impact that have delayed or ignored security updates. They can also leverage the platform's reliance on continuous visibility and threat intelligence to identify gaps in its detection capabilities, enabling them to craft sophisticated attacks that evade initial scrutiny.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries may prioritize targeting systems based on risk scoring to maximize damage and exploit opportunities where security updates have been delayed or ignored due to perceived low risk.
- Attackers can use the platform's reliance on continuous visibility against it by crafting threats that mimic benign activities, thereby evading detection mechanisms.
- Threat actors might leverage third-party EDR integration weaknesses within Falcon Next-Gen SIEM to introduce malicious tools undetected.
- Adversaries could exploit gaps in attack path analysis and validation processes to conduct multi-stage attacks that circumvent risk prioritization measures.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
The CrowdStrike Falcon Platform leverages AI-driven analytics and dynamic risk-based vulnerability management to help organizations comply with CISA BOD-26-04, while also facing potential adversary exploitation through targeted attacks on high-risk assets and evasion tactics.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use sophisticated attack strategies and evasion techniques to exploit the dynamic risk-based vulnerability management system of CrowdStrike Falcon Platform in order to maximize damage on critical assets with delayed security updates.

### Gaps Identified
- There is limited coverage for detecting threats that mimic benign activities or leverage third-party EDR integration weaknesses within Falcon Next-Gen SIEM, potentially allowing adversaries to introduce malicious tools undetected.
- The current implementation of telemetry mapping might not fully capture all necessary logs for timely identification and remediation of vulnerabilities related to critical software components like Log4j, potentially leaving organizations non-compliant with CISA BOD-26-04 mandates.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use sophisticated attack strategies and evasion techniques to exploit the dynamic risk-based vulnerability management system of CrowdStrike Falcon Platform in order to maximize damage on critical assets with delayed security updates.
- Gap: There is limited coverage for detecting threats that mimic benign activities or leverage third-party EDR integration weaknesses within Falcon Next-Gen SIEM, potentially allowing adversaries to introduce malicious tools undetected.
- Focus: Enhancing the detection of threats that evade initial scrutiny by crafting behaviors that mimic normal operations and identifying gaps in attack path analysis processes.
- Gap: The current implementation of telemetry mapping might not fully capture all necessary logs for timely identification and remediation of vulnerabilities related to critical software components like Log4j, potentially leaving organizations non-compliant with CISA BOD-26-04 mandates.
- Focus: Improving the automation and real-time monitoring capabilities of vulnerability scans and security event logs to ensure comprehensive coverage in compliance with government mandates.
