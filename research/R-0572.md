## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Passwordless authentication systems typically rely on alternative methods such as biometrics, security keys (like USB tokens), or one-time codes sent via email or SMS to verify user identity without the need for a password. These mechanisms aim to enhance security and usability by eliminating the vulnerability associated with weak or stolen passwords.

### Key Findings
- Passwordless authentication systems often utilize multifactor authentication methods to ensure that users are who they claim to be, even in the absence of traditional passwords.
- [UNCERTAIN] The reliance on hardware tokens or biometric data can introduce new vulnerabilities if not properly secured; for example, a compromised hardware token could bypass any additional security measures.
- Users must trust the service provider with sensitive information like IP addresses and device identifiers to ensure that authentication requests are coming from known devices.
- One-time codes sent via SMS are considered less secure than other methods due to potential interception or SIM swapping attacks.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries can exploit the 'Passkey' system by leveraging phishing attacks, credential stuffing, or social engineering tactics to gain unauthorized access. They may also manipulate user interfaces (UI) on devices to capture authentication data without raising suspicion.

### Key Findings
- Phishing and social engineering are effective in tricking users into providing their Passkeys to malicious parties.
- [UNCERTAIN] Attackers could exploit vulnerabilities in the biometric or security key infrastructure used by Passkey systems to bypass authentication mechanisms.
- Credential stuffing attacks can be adapted to target Passkey services, leveraging stolen credentials from other breaches.
- Adversaries might create rogue applications that mimic legitimate ones, tricking users into authenticating with their Passkeys and thereby compromising account security.
- Manipulating UIs on devices or web pages to capture user inputs during the authentication process is a viable tactic for stealing Passkeys without direct interaction.
- [UNCERTAIN] There's potential for exploitation through side-channel attacks that monitor user behavior patterns in biometric authentication processes.
