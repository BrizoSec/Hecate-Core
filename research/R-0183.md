## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
ValleyRAT infection campaigns utilize deceptive tactics such as fake installers that mimic legitimate software to distribute malware. This approach helps evade detection by establishing persistence within the system's registry keys and exploiting code signing mechanisms.

### Key Findings
- The ValleyRAT campaign uses misleading installer packages to trick users into downloading and installing malicious payloads.
- Malware establishes persistence on infected systems by modifying registry keys, ensuring it can reinfect or maintain a foothold even after removal attempts.
- ValleyRAT employs code signing abuse techniques to appear more legitimate and evade detection by security software.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
The Silver Fox group has been distributing ValleyRAT and ABCDoor backdoors through fake installer files in phishing campaigns, primarily targeting organizations in Russia and India. These attacks often involve emails that impersonate tax authorities to trick victims into downloading the malicious installers.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries use social engineering tactics via phishing emails to distribute ValleyRAT and ABCDoor backdoors
- The fake installer files are disguised as legitimate software updates or security tools to lure victims
- Targeted organizations in Russia and India have been the primary focus of these campaigns
- Phishing emails often masquerade as communications from tax authorities, enhancing their credibility among potential victims
- ValleyRAT allows adversaries to gain remote access and control over compromised systems
- ABCDoor serves as a backdoor tool enabling persistent access and lateral movement within networks
