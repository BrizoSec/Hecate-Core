## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Fake AI tools masquerading as legitimate software are used by threat actors to deliver infostealers on macOS and other platforms. These fake utilities trick users into downloading malicious payloads that steal sensitive information such as passwords and financial data.

### Key Findings
- Threat actors utilize fake AI-themed applications to lure victims into downloading malware.
- Once executed, these tools communicate with attacker-controlled servers to receive further instructions or download additional payloads.
- Infostealers are often delivered via deceptive web pages that mimic legitimate software interfaces and require user interaction (e.g., clicking 'Continue') to initiate the infection process.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are using fake AI software updates and phishing campaigns to distribute infostealers like LummaC2, taking advantage of the public's increasing trust in AI tools. This method lowers technical barriers for malware creation and accelerates the proliferation of sophisticated information-stealing malware.

### Key Findings
- Malware distributors create brand-impersonating GitHub repositories to trick users into downloading malicious software updates.
- Infostealers such as LummaC2 are delivered through various initial access vectors, including phishing emails and fake update notifications.
- The use of AI in malware development reduces the technical skill level required for creating complex threats, leading to an increase in functional malware variants.
- RMM (Remote Monitoring and Management) tools are being used by threat actors as a precursor to ransomware or other malicious activities, often after initial compromise through phishing campaigns.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Threat actors are leveraging fake AI-themed applications and phishing campaigns to distribute infostealers such as LummaC2, exploiting the public's trust in AI technology. These tools often communicate with attacker-controlled servers post-execution for further instructions or additional payload delivery.

### Recommended Hypothesis
