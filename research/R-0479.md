## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Botnets operate by infecting multiple systems to form a network of compromised machines, which are then used to carry out various malicious activities such as data theft and spam distribution. These networks employ sophisticated evasion techniques like fast flux DNS and encrypted communication channels to avoid detection.

### Key Findings
- Botnet operators use advanced tactics like fast flux DNS to hide their command-and-control servers and evade law enforcement.
- Modern botnets often feature modular architectures that allow attackers to easily update or expand the capabilities of infected systems without re-infecting them.
- Sophisticated encryption is used in botnet communication to protect data transferred between compromised machines and the control server.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries abuse the botnet technique by compromising numerous systems to form a network that can be instructed to perform coordinated attacks such as DDoS, phishing campaigns, and data exfiltration. Botnets are often used to distribute malware or launch other malicious activities on a large scale.

### Key Findings
- Botnets grow through the distribution of malware that targets vulnerabilities in systems and networks
- Adversaries use command-and-control (C&C) servers to manage botnet operations, including issuing instructions for various attacks
- Techniques such as ArcaneDoor involve intercepting HTTP traffic to steal C&C information and control compromised devices
- Botnets are increasingly sophisticated with the ability to self-propagate and dynamically update their malware components
- Detection of botnet activities is challenging due to the use of encryption, domain generation algorithms (DGAs), and fast-flux networks

## 3. Telemetry Mapping: OCSF Fields

### Summary
Telemetry for detecting an evolving botnet should capture network traffic anomalies, especially those indicative of command-and-control (C2) communication and lateral movement within a compromised network. This includes monitoring outbound connections to known malicious IP addresses or domains, unexpected DNS requests, and unusual patterns in process creation and inter-process communications.

### Key Fields
- network.dns.query.name: Monitors for suspicious domain names that may be used by botnets for C2 communication (e.g., short-lived domains).
- network.http.request.uri: Detects HTTP/HTTPS requests to malicious URLs or IP addresses indicative of data exfiltration.
- process.executable.path: Tracks the execution paths and binaries being run, which can indicate malware activities such as payload delivery or lateral movement.
- event.4688 (Windows Event ID): Captures process creation events that may be used by bots to establish persistence mechanisms or spread laterally within a network.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Botnets are evolving with sophisticated evasion techniques, modular architectures, and encrypted communications to avoid detection. They leverage command-and-control servers for large-scale attacks such as DDoS and data exfiltration.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use advanced encryption and fast flux DNS tactics to evade detection while deploying botnets that self-propagate and dynamically update malware components in order to launch coordinated attacks on unsuspecting targets.

### Gaps Identified
- Current telemetry lacks comprehensive monitoring for the dynamic nature of botnet communication, particularly around encrypted channels and modular updates within compromised systems.

- Hypothesis: Adversaries use advanced encryption and fast flux DNS tactics to evade detection while deploying botnets that self-propagate and dynamically update malware components in order to launch coordinated attacks on unsuspecting targets.
