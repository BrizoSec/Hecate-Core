## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Operation RoundPress involves the exploitation of half-click webmail zero-days, where attackers convert use-after-free vulnerabilities into read/write primitives to execute ROP chains and hooks. This enables them to deploy shellcode for malicious purposes, highlighting a significant threat to enterprise software security.

### Key Findings
- Attackers are increasingly leveraging use-after-free vulnerabilities in webmail systems by converting them into full control over memory using ROP chains and hooks.
- The exploitation technique known as 'half-click' allows attackers to gain access with minimal user interaction, thereby increasing the stealthiness of their attacks.
- There is a notable rise in the public disclosure of n-day vulnerabilities that are subsequently exploited widely by threat actors.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries involved in Operation RoundPress are utilizing T1005, Data from Local System, to gather sensitive information and identify files of interest before exfiltrating data. They employ advanced techniques such as webshells and SSH tunnels for lateral movement within targeted networks.

### Key Findings
- Use of half-click webmail zero-days to gain initial access and deploy BumbleBee Webshell
- Employment of T1005 technique to read configuration files like web.config for credential harvesting
