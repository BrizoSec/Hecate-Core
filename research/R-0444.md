## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
GoldPickaxe is an iOS Trojan that steals biometric data, such as facial recognition information, from users through fake government apps. It exploits Mobile Device Management (MDM) to gain control over devices and uses the stolen biometric data for identity theft by creating deepfakes.

### Key Findings
- GoldPickaxe spreads via fraudulent government applications that trick users into providing their biometric data through various verification methods like short videos or document uploads.
- The trojan leverages MDM capabilities to take full control of the device, allowing it to access and exfiltrate sensitive information.
- Stolen biometric data is used to create deepfake identities for identity theft purposes.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries use the technique of obfuscating files or information (T1406) to evade detection by security systems, often employing methods such as encryption and steganography. This allows them to hide malicious code within seemingly benign files.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries employ non-standard ports for network traffic generation to avoid common monitoring tools, complicating detection efforts (T1509).
- Steganographic techniques are a sub-technique of T1406 where adversaries embed malicious payloads within images or other files without obvious signs.
- Biometric systems face threats from limited revocation options and deep fake attacks, making biometric data as vulnerable to theft and exploitation as financial information.
- Attackers often use encryption to obfuscate the content of their malicious files, hindering analysis by security researchers and incident responders.
- The wide spread of biometric credentials increases the potential impact of a successful attack on such systems.
- MITRE ATT&CK framework updates highlight the evolution in defense evasion tactics, emphasizing the need for continuous adaptation in cybersecurity strategies.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
GoldPickaxe is a sophisticated iOS Trojan that steals biometric data through fraudulent government apps and exploits MDM capabilities to control devices, using the stolen information for identity theft via deepfakes. Adversaries employ various obfuscation techniques such as encryption and steganography to evade detection.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use social engineering and obfuscation techniques to steal biometric data for identity theft purposes on iOS devices through fraudulent government apps.

### Gaps Identified
- Current security measures lack effective methods to detect and respond to the use of steganography in embedding malicious payloads within benign files, as well as encryption used by adversaries to hide their activities.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use social engineering and obfuscation techniques to steal biometric data for identity theft purposes on iOS devices through fraudulent government apps.
- Gap: Current security measures lack effective methods to detect and respond to the use of steganography in embedding malicious payloads within benign files, as well as encryption used by adversaries to hide their activities.
- Focus: Improve detection mechanisms for anomalous access patterns to biometric data storage locations and network traffic anomalies indicative of data exfiltration. Additionally, enhance user education on recognizing fraudulent government apps and the risks associated with biometric data theft.
