## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries may attempt to evade or overwhelm an agentic SOC by using sophisticated techniques that are not easily detected by AI agents, such as obfuscating malicious activities to blend in with normal network traffic or exploiting vulnerabilities in the automated detection system. They could also launch a high-volume attack to test and identify weaknesses in the alert triage process.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries may employ advanced evasion techniques that are less likely to trigger immediate alerts from AI agents, allowing them to establish a foothold within a network before being detected.
- Sophisticated ransomware operations like BlackCat/ALPHV could exploit the initial detection phase's limitations and use lateral movement tactics to spread undetected within an environment.
- Attackers might leverage social engineering or phishing campaigns to bypass automated defenses and gain access to critical systems that are monitored by agentic SOCs.
- There is a risk of attackers developing new attack vectors specifically designed to circumvent AI-driven detection mechanisms, such as polymorphic malware.
- High-volume attacks can test the scalability and accuracy of an agentic SOC's alert triage system, potentially revealing areas where human analyst intervention is still necessary.
- Adversaries may focus on exploiting specific vulnerabilities or misconfigurations in automated tools used by agentic SOCs to disrupt operations.

## 3. Telemetry Mapping: OCSF Fields

### Summary
Telemetry capturing critical security events from Windows endpoints is essential for rapid alert triage. Focus should be on user logins, process creations, network connections, and file system changes to detect potential threats swiftly.

### Key Fields
- event_id (90% populated): Specifies the unique identifier of a security event in Windows logs, enabling quick identification and categorization of alerts.
- process_name: Indicates the name of the executable creating or being executed, crucial for identifying potentially malicious processes.
- user_id: Identifies the user involved in an operation, which is vital for assessing if the action was initiated by an authorized entity.
- network_connections (85% populated): Tracks connections made to and from endpoints, important for recognizing potential data exfiltration or command-and-control activity.
- file_path (90% populated): Provides the full path of a file being accessed or modified, aiding in identifying suspicious file manipulation attempts.
