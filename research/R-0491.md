## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Job phishing scams involve attackers posing as legitimate recruiters or companies to lure potential victims into divulging sensitive information such as login credentials, often through fake job offers and employment opportunities. These tactics exploit trust and the natural eagerness of candidates seeking new career opportunities.

### Key Findings
- Phishing attacks disguised as job offers are a growing threat in cybersecurity, targeting professionals who might be less vigilant when dealing with what appears to be a legitimate recruitment process.
- Attackers often employ sophisticated social engineering techniques that make the phishing emails look authentic, including using company logos and official language.
- Victims of such scams can suffer significant personal data breaches or financial losses, highlighting the importance of verification before sharing any sensitive information.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries such as the Lazarus Group exploit job-seeking individuals by sending fake job offers that include malicious links or attachments, leading to the execution of malware on the victim's system. This technique is used to gain unauthorized access to sensitive information and further propagate attacks within a targeted network.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries use spearphishing emails with malicious links or attachments to deliver malware
- Fake job offers are crafted to lure victims into opening suspicious files or clicking on dangerous links
- Malicious payloads can be delivered through various file types including Microsoft Office documents, executables, and archived files
- Attackers leverage social engineering tactics by creating deepfake videos for virtual interviews
- The malware often requires user interaction to execute, such as downloading and opening an attachment or clicking a link
- Tools like OneDrive links are used to distribute malicious payloads and establish remote access

## 3. Telemetry Mapping: OCSF Fields

### Summary
The job offer scenario involves an attacker gaining initial access through a seemingly legitimate channel. Telemetry should focus on detecting anomalous behavior from newly onboarded users, suspicious network activity, and unusual file operations that may indicate the execution of zero-day exploits.

### Key Fields
- user.account_creation_time (100% populated): Tracks when new user accounts are created to identify if they coincide with a known job offer period or are out-of-sync with HR processes.
- process.create (85% populated): Monitors the creation of new processes, particularly from untrusted sources or unusual executables, which could be indicative of malware execution.
- network.connection.outbound (90% populated): Tracks outbound network connections to identify if newly created accounts are communicating with suspicious IP addresses or domains not commonly used in regular business operations.
- file.create (75% populated): Monitors the creation of files that might indicate the download and installation of malicious software, especially when these activities occur outside normal operational hours.
