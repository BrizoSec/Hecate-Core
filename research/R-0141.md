## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Akira ransomware employs double-extortion tactics by encrypting data using advanced encryption methods (ChaCha20 and RSA-4096) and threatening to publish stolen data unless a ransom is paid. It is written in Rust, allowing for efficient targeted file encryption.

### Key Findings
- Akira uses double-extortion techniques involving both encryption and exfiltration of data.
- The ransomware employs robust encryption methods like ChaCha20 and RSA-4096 to secure files.
- Written in Rust, Akira benefits from the language's performance advantages for file encryption tasks.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are using the Bumblebee malware as an initial access tool, often delivered through trojanized MSI installers via malicious Bing search results or other social engineering techniques. This leads to the deployment of Akira ransomware, which encrypts local and network shares causing significant disruption.

### Key Findings
- Bumblebee malware is used for initial access, typically delivered via trojanized MSI installers.
- Remote access tools like RustDesk are employed after initial infection to move laterally within the network.
- Akira ransomware encrypts both local and network shares, causing significant data loss and operational disruption.
- Threat actors use discovery scripts such as Invoke-ShareFinder and DaptoHunter for reconnaissance.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Adversaries are employing a multi-stage attack methodology involving Bumblebee malware for initial access and lateral movement, followed by the deployment of Akira ransomware that leverages advanced encryption techniques to extort victims. This attack chain often starts from malicious Bing search results leading to trojanized MSI installers.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use trojanized MSI installers and social engineering tactics to deliver Bumblebee malware, subsequently deploying Akira ransomware with the goal of extorting victims through data encryption and exfiltration.

### Gaps Identified
- Current telemetry may not adequately capture lateral movement activities such as Remote Desktop Protocol (RDP) sessions or other remote access tools used by adversaries post-infection.
- There is limited visibility into reconnaissance activities like the use of PowerShell scripts for network discovery, which could help in early detection and mitigation of Akira ransomware attacks.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use trojanized MSI installers and social engineering tactics to deliver Bumblebee malware, subsequently deploying Akira ransomware with the goal of extorting victims through data encryption and exfiltration.
- Gap: Current telemetry may not adequately capture lateral movement activities such as Remote Desktop Protocol (RDP) sessions or other remote access tools used by adversaries post-infection.
- Gap: There is limited visibility into reconnaissance activities like the use of PowerShell scripts for network discovery, which could help in early detection and mitigation of Akira ransomware attacks.
- Focus: Enhance SIEM rules to detect unusual MSI installer executions and outbound connections to known C2 servers associated with Bumblebee malware.
- Focus: Investigate and monitor potential lateral movement techniques such as RDP sessions or other remote access tools that adversaries might use after initial infection.
