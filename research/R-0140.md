## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
An insider threat refers to risks posed by individuals within an organization who might misuse their access rights intentionally or unintentionally, potentially leading to data theft or sabotage. Management of such threats involves comprehensive training and ongoing monitoring.

### Key Findings
- Insider threats can originate from current employees, former employees, contractors, or business associates with internal knowledge about the system.
- Effective management of insider threats includes regular security awareness training for all staff members.
- Monitoring activities and access controls are crucial in detecting potential insider threat behaviors.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries exploit shell evasion techniques to disguise malicious activities as benign operations, thereby evading detection mechanisms and maintaining undetected access within targeted systems. These methods often include privilege escalation and defense evasion tactics to ensure prolonged control over the compromised environment.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries use sophisticated shell evasion techniques to mask their presence and actions on a network.
- Attackers employ privilege escalation to gain higher levels of access, facilitating broader system compromise.
- Financially motivated adversaries target users in specific regions with campaigns like Qakbot, utilizing old email threads for disguise.
- Backdoors are employed as part of an attack strategy to maintain persistent access and control over compromised systems.
- Adversaries leverage active attack vectors that alter or disrupt system operations, often targeting critical infrastructure and data.
- The ATT&CK framework provides detailed insights into the tactics, techniques, and procedures used by adversaries during cyberattacks.
