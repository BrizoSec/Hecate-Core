## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Microsoft 365 Calendars allow users to share calendar access with others, both within and outside their organization, by setting appropriate permissions. This sharing mechanism can also be exploited for covert command-and-control communication channels using seemingly innocuous calendar events.

### Key Findings
- Users can grant different levels of permission (e.g., view, edit) to other users or external parties for accessing their calendars.
- PowerShell scripts and similar tools enable enumeration of all calendar permissions assigned to a user within an organization.
- Malicious actors can leverage shared calendar events as a covert channel to exfiltrate data or issue commands by embedding metadata in event descriptions.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are exploiting Microsoft 365 Calendars by embedding malicious commands within seemingly benign calendar event invitations, turning them into covert command-and-control channels. This technique allows attackers to communicate with compromised systems without raising suspicion.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries use specially crafted iCalendar (ICS) files embedded in email invitations or shared calendar entries to deliver commands to infected devices.
- Malicious calendar events can contain hidden metadata or payloads that trigger actions on the recipient's system, such as downloading additional malware or executing specific instructions.
- Indicators of Compromise (IoCs) include unusual network traffic patterns initiated by calendar applications, unexpected external connections made during typical office hours when no meetings are scheduled, and the presence of suspicious calendar entries with obfuscated details or hidden content.
- Attackers leverage the trust users have in their email clients and calendar applications to bypass standard security measures and evade detection.

## 3. Telemetry Mapping: OCSF Fields

### Summary
To detect unauthorized use of Microsoft 365 calendars for command-and-control purposes, the following telemetry should be collected: anomalies in calendar event creation or modification times, unusual patterns of repeated scheduling changes, unexpected external senders in email correspondence related to calendar invites, and network traffic indicative of data exfiltration through calendar events.

### Key Fields
- user_activity.calendar_event_create (15% populated): Tracks the creation of new calendar events by users. Suspicious activity may include frequent event creations or modifications around off-hours.
- email.message.sender_email_address: Identifies email addresses associated with sender emails related to calendar invites. Unusual external senders should be flagged for further investigation.
- network.http_request.uri (70% populated): Captures URIs accessed by users' browsers or applications, which may include URLs embedded in calendar event descriptions used as command-and-control channels.
- user_activity.email_opened: Tracks when and how often emails containing calendar invites are opened. Unexpectedly high open rates from suspicious senders should be monitored.
