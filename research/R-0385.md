## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are using a Remote Access Trojan (RAT) that leverages legitimate browser processes to establish and maintain command-and-control (C2) communications without directly utilizing the network, thereby evading traditional detection mechanisms. This technique highlights the importance of prioritizing specific threat vectors rather than attempting comprehensive coverage.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries are employing RATs that evade detection by routing C2 traffic through legitimate browser processes, exploiting living-off-the-land (LoTL) techniques to blend in with normal network activity.
- The abuse of web browsers for malicious purposes demonstrates the need for organizations to focus on detecting and mitigating specific, targeted threats rather than generic ones.
- Detection mechanisms must be refined to identify anomalous behavior within legitimate applications, such as unexpected HTTP requests or DNS queries that suggest C2 communications.
- Organizations should prioritize threat intelligence to understand unique attack vectors relevant to their environment before attempting broad-based defense strategies.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Adversaries are utilizing Remote Access Trojans (RATs) that exploit legitimate browser processes to evade detection, highlighting the need for organizations to prioritize specific threat vectors rather than attempting comprehensive coverage. Additionally, RDP-related telemetry suggests potential lateral movement within networks, indicating the importance of monitoring user activities and network traffic.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use RATs exploiting legitimate browser processes to evade detection mechanisms in order to establish command-and-control communications on targeted systems.

### Gaps Identified
- Current detection methods may not be sufficient for identifying anomalous behavior within legitimate applications such as web browsers, leading to potential evasion of traditional security measures.

### Key Findings
