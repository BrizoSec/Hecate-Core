## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Aeternum leverages blockchain technology, specifically Polygon smart contracts, to establish a persistent Command and Control (C2) infrastructure for its malware operations. This innovative approach enables the group to maintain long-term control over compromised systems while evading traditional detection mechanisms.

### Key Findings
- Aeternum uses blockchain-based C2 channels via Polygon smart contracts to execute and manage malicious activities.
- The RDAT backdoor is employed by Aeternum to gain initial access and persist on victim networks.
- Custom Mimikatz tools are utilized to escalate privileges within compromised systems, facilitating deeper reconnaissance and data exfiltration.
- Palo Alto Networks’ WildFire, Cortex XDR, and URL Filtering solutions provide protection against Aeternum's tactics by detecting and mitigating its malicious activities.

### Executive Summary
Malware developers are increasingly leveraging blockchain technology, particularly Polygon smart contracts and custom tools like RDAT backdoor and Mimikatz variants, to create persistent C2 infrastructures that evade traditional security measures. Telemetry should focus on monitoring network communications related to blockchain nodes/services, DNS requests to malicious addresses, HTTP/HTTPS traffic associated with C2 commands through blockchain APIs, and execution of binaries linked to cryptocurrency wallets or explorers.

### Recommended Hypothesis
