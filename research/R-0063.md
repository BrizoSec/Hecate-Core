## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
The Portugal-focused phishing campaign employs multi-stage malware and adversary-in-the-middle (AiTM) attacks to bypass multi-factor authentication (MFA). These sophisticated AiTM attacks impersonate legitimate services like Booking.com, steal credentials, and are detected and mitigated by Microsoft's security tools through third-party network detection.

### Key Findings
- The campaign leverages AiTM techniques to intercept and manipulate communication between users and trusted services, effectively bypassing MFA mechanisms.
- Microsoft’s security products play a crucial role in identifying these sophisticated phishing attempts by analyzing patterns of network activity indicative of AiTM attacks.
- Third-party network detection is essential for uncovering the multi-stage nature of malware delivered through such campaigns, allowing for early mitigation and prevention.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are leveraging an AI-assisted phishing toolkit to target Portuguese victims, deploying WebDAV and HollowGraph malware through hijacked servers and exploiting Microsoft 365 services. The malware hides command-and-control (C2) communication and stolen files within future-dated calendar events in Microsoft 365, making detection challenging.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries are using an AI-assisted phishing toolkit to create convincing social engineering campaigns targeting Portuguese users.
- The campaign exploits hijacked servers and WebDAV protocol to distribute malware that targets Microsoft 365 services.
- HollowGraph malware is used to hide C2 communications and stolen data inside future-dated calendar events, complicating forensic analysis and detection efforts.
- Phishing campaigns are delivering multistage malware, with the initial stage likely being a phishing email leading to malicious web pages or documents exploiting WebDAV.
