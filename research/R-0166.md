## 1. System Research: How It Works

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Lateral movement is a technique employed by cyber adversaries after gaining initial access to a network, allowing them to navigate and escalate privileges within the network in search of valuable data or systems. This involves transferring tools and files between compromised hosts to maintain persistence and control.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries use credential dumping and pass-the-ticket techniques to leverage existing credentials for lateral movement.
- Fileless malware is often employed to evade detection by residing entirely in memory, using legitimate Windows management tools like PsExec or WMI.
- Unusual network traffic patterns, such as unexpected SMB (Server Message Block) or RDP (Remote Desktop Protocol) connections, are indicators of lateral movement.
- Lateral tool transfer involves the copying of malicious binaries and scripts between compromised systems to support further exploitation activities.
