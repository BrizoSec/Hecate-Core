## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries use botnets to coordinate large-scale attacks such as phishing campaigns and DDoS, leveraging compromised systems to perform malicious activities on a massive scale. The Dysphoria adversary is noted for its evolving tactics in botnet creation and control.

### Key Findings
- Botnets are often used to carry out coordinated distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks, overwhelming targets with traffic from numerous infected machines.
- Adversaries compromise systems through various methods such as phishing emails containing malicious payloads or exploiting vulnerabilities in software.
- The use of sophisticated encryption and compression techniques, like those seen in the Prometei botnet's code structure, indicates a high level of technical sophistication among attackers.
- Multi-factor authentication interception tools are employed to bypass security measures on compromised systems, allowing adversaries greater control over botnet infrastructure.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
The Dysphoria botnet is an advanced threat employing sophisticated techniques such as encrypted communications, system vulnerability exploitation, and multi-factor authentication interception to evade detection while coordinating large-scale attacks. Key IoCs include monitoring of process creation, network connections, file modifications, and registry accesses.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use sophisticated encryption and lateral movement tactics to establish persistent botnet infrastructure on compromised systems for the goal of conducting large-scale DDoS attacks targeting various organizations.

### Gaps Identified
- There is limited understanding of how Dysphoria evades advanced endpoint detection and response (EDR) solutions, particularly in relation to its use of multi-factor authentication interception tools.
- Current telemetry lacks comprehensive visibility into the initial infection vectors, making it difficult to trace back and understand the full scope of the botnet's infiltration strategy.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use sophisticated encryption and lateral movement tactics to establish persistent botnet infrastructure on compromised systems for the goal of conducting large-scale DDoS attacks targeting various organizations.
- Gap: There is limited understanding of how Dysphoria evades advanced endpoint detection and response (EDR) solutions, particularly in relation to its use of multi-factor authentication interception tools.
- Gap: Current telemetry lacks comprehensive visibility into the initial infection vectors, making it difficult to trace back and understand the full scope of the botnet's infiltration strategy.
- Focus: Enhance network security monitoring by implementing deeper inspection of outbound communications to identify encrypted C2 traffic patterns specific to Dysphoria.
- Focus: Develop more robust incident response protocols for detecting and mitigating multi-factor authentication interception tools used by adversaries.
