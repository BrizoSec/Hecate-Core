## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries often insert malicious code or components into the software supply chain to gain unauthorized access, maintain persistence, and exfiltrate data from targeted organizations. This technique exploits the trust relationship between vendors and their customers, making detection challenging due to the trusted nature of the compromised components.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries commonly target open-source projects or widely-used software libraries where modifications can reach a large number of users with minimal effort on the attacker's part.
- [UNCERTAIN] Malicious implants may include backdoors, RATs (Remote Access Trojans), and cryptocurrency miners that are designed to remain undetected for extended periods.
- Attackers leverage signed binaries or code signing certificates stolen from legitimate software vendors to distribute malware through trusted channels.
- Indicators of supply chain compromise can be subtle, such as unexpected changes in software versions, anomalous communications with known malicious IP addresses or domains, and unusual file hashes or signatures.
- Organizations should implement robust verification processes for third-party components and conduct regular audits of their software dependencies to detect anomalies early.
- [UNCERTAIN] Some sophisticated attacks involve the compromise of build systems or repositories to inject malware at a very early stage in the development cycle.
