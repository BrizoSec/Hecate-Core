## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries execute a confidence trick by offering unsolicited consultancy or job opportunities to security professionals, using initial low-stakes consultations to screen for targets with valuable privileged access. If the target passes this screening, attackers manipulate them via professional flattery into abusing their trusted relationships and internal systems to deliver sensitive, non-public reports.

### Key Findings
- Attackers utilize sparse social media profiles claiming to be consultants or recruiters, often lacking a digital footprint for their supposed employer, to initiate unsolicited contact with security professionals.
- The initial engagement typically involves a paid phone consultation (e.g., $300) on topics like digital transformation, serving primarily as a screening process to verify the target's access level and knowledge.
- Success in the screening leads to requests for written reports or 'special reports' that require the victim to actively probe internal systems, reach out to colleagues, or leverage professional relationships.
- The core exploit is psychological, targeting security practitioners' overconfidence and susceptibility to professional flattery to induce them to compromise their own professional integrity and trusted industry relationships.
- Variant tactics include fake recruiters offering prestigious positions that require the installation of trojanised software under the pretense of onboarding or system compatibility.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Adversaries execute a confidence trick by leveraging unsolicited social media profiles offering paid consultancy or recruitment to screen for security professionals with privileged access. The technical execution culminates in the distribution of trojanized software under the guise of legitimate tools, establishing persistent C2 implants and facilitating data exfiltration through abused professional channels.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use fake consultancy offers and trojanized recruitment software to gain initial access and abuse trusted insider relationships for sensitive data collection.

### Gaps Identified
- Lack of telemetry correlating outbound social media activity with subsequent endpoint process executions or network connections to unknown IPs, leaving the 'screening' phase invisible to traditional perimeter defenses.
- Inability to detect 'living off the land' behaviors where attackers induce victims to use legitimate administrative tools (e.g., PowerShell, sysinternals) or professional communication channels (e.g., Slack, Teams) for unauthorized probing and exfiltration.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use fake consultancy offers and trojanized recruitment software to gain initial access and abuse trusted insider relationships for sensitive data collection.
- Gap: Lack of telemetry correlating outbound social media activity with subsequent endpoint process executions or network connections to unknown IPs, leaving the 'screening' phase invisible to traditional perimeter defenses.
- Gap: Inability to detect 'living off the land' behaviors where attackers induce victims to use legitimate administrative tools (e.g., PowerShell, sysinternals) or professional communication channels (e.g., Slack, Teams) for unauthorized probing and exfiltration.
- Focus: Hunt for newly installed executables with suspicious signing chains or unusual parent-child process relationships initiated by browser or email clients during business hours.
- Focus: Monitor for outbound connections from workstations to non-standard ports or domains associated with known C2 infrastructure, specifically targeting endpoints belonging to security teams or privileged accounts.
