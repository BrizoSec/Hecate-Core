## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
A cyber-operation involving actors possibly affiliated with China was identified through a breach in their OPSEC. The mistake allowed researchers to trace the operation's infrastructure and identify key indicators of compromise (IoCs) that could be used for defensive purposes.

### Key Findings
- The operation utilized compromised systems within China as staging points, indicating possible state-sponsored involvement or at least internal cooperation with actors operating from within Chinese borders.
- Communication between command and control servers and compromised machines was not properly encrypted, allowing network traffic analysis to reveal patterns indicative of the operation's tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs).
- Malware samples associated with the operation included hardcoded strings or IP addresses that were registered under real-world identities, providing direct links back to potential actors.
- Use of common tools and software frameworks known for their association with Chinese APT groups suggests a pattern of behavior consistent with other known operations attributed to China.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries associated with the JadeProx operation use encrypted or encoded files to obfuscate malicious content and evade detection, often embedding phishing payloads within password-protected documents or self-extracting archives. This technique is part of a broader strategy that includes hijacking execution flows through DLL sideloading via signed binaries and masquerading as legitimate software.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries use encrypted/encoded files to hide malicious content, such as phishing payloads in password-protected Word documents or self-extracting archives.
- JadeProx employs the TriBack loader, a new malware variant used for cyberespionage against government and healthcare organizations.
- The operation leverages signed binaries to sideload malicious DLLs, hijacking execution flows to bypass security measures.
- Attackers masquerade their activities by using file names that match legitimate software names (e.g., hostfxr.dll) to blend in with normal system processes.

The operation employs various obfuscation techniques including encrypted/encoded files and DLL sideloading through signed binaries to evade detection.
