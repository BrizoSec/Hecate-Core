## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
AI-assisted development enables threat actors to rapidly create and deploy malicious tools, enhancing their efficiency in reconnaissance, exploitation, and post-exploitation stages. AI tools automate workflows for malware development and execution, while custom frameworks generated by AI are used for large-scale network recon.

### Key Findings
- Threat actors use low-code AI platforms to develop and deploy malicious tools quickly.
- AI-generated reconnaissance frameworks help in the large-scale access and exploitation of networks post-VPN deployment.
- Malware development processes are accelerated using AI, supporting iterative engineering tasks throughout malware lifecycle.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are increasingly leveraging AI-driven models to automate attack processes, conduct reconnaissance more efficiently, and generate convincing content for social engineering attacks. This integration not only lowers the barrier to entry for less skilled attackers but also enhances the operational efficiency of sophisticated threat actors by automating tasks such as drafting phishing emails or generating malware.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries are using publicly available large language models (LLMs) and AI-driven tools to automate reconnaissance, exploit development, and content creation processes for attacks.
- AI is being integrated into post-compromise operations by threat actors, particularly those who speak Chinese, to enhance operational efficiency in targeting Windows and Linux web servers globally across various sectors including government and technology.
- The use of agentic AI models enables adversaries to perform autonomous attack cycles with reduced human intervention, increasing the speed and scale of attacks while lowering the margin for error.
- AI is being used to generate plausible but malicious URLs (phantom squatting) which can be preemptively registered by attackers to lure victims into compromising their systems.
- Prompt injection has emerged as a dominant method where adversaries strategically insert malicious prompts in locations accessed by AI agents, hijacking the generated content for malicious purposes.
- Adversaries are employing AI not only to develop new attack vectors but also to refine existing tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs), making them more efficient and harder to detect.

## 3. Telemetry Mapping: OCSF Fields

### Summary
Telemetry capturing AI-assisted development and persistent threat operations should focus on changes in access patterns to code repositories, execution of scripts or binaries from unexpected locations, network communications with suspicious IPs or domains, and anomalies in user behavior such as unusual hours of activity.

### Key Fields
- code_repository_access (50% populated): Tracks who accessed which code repository files, including timestamps and details on what changes were made. This is crucial for detecting unauthorized access or modifications to source code by AI systems or malicious actors.
- process_creation (80% populated): Logs the creation of processes across endpoints, including parent-child process relationships and associated user accounts. Useful for identifying scripts or binaries run from unusual locations that could indicate automated development activities or lateral movement techniques used in persistent threat operations.
- network_traffic (75% populated): Captures details about network connections established by endpoint devices, including source/destination IP addresses and domain names. This is key to detecting C2 communications with known malicious infrastructure.
- user_activity (60% populated): Tracks user logins, logout events, and other activities indicating normal vs. anomalous behavior patterns. Sudden changes in user activity levels or hours can suggest automated access by AI systems or persistent threat actors.
- file_modification (70% populated): Logs modifications to files on endpoints, including timestamps and file paths for tracking changes made to sensitive directories or system configurations that might indicate malicious intent.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Threat actors are leveraging AI-assisted development platforms to accelerate and automate the creation of malicious tools, enhancing their operational efficiency in reconnaissance, exploitation, and post-exploitation phases. This shift towards more automated attack cycles reduces human intervention and increases both the speed and scale of persistent threat operations.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use AI-driven models to automate and enhance reconnaissance, exploit development, and social engineering attacks to efficiently target Windows and Linux web servers across various sectors.

### Gaps Identified
- Current telemetry capturing does not fully account for the nuances in detecting automated access patterns by AI systems or unusual user behavior that could indicate malicious activity. Additionally, there is a lack of comprehensive understanding of how agentic AI models are employed in autonomous attack cycles.
