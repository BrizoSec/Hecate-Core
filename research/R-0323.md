## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
The AI supply chain involves a complex network of foundational code libraries, interactive computing applications, data infrastructure tools, and vector embedding databases that collectively support the development and deployment of AI models. Compromising this ecosystem can lead to severe consequences such as model manipulation, sensitive data extraction, and misuse of AI-powered systems.

### Key Findings
- The AI supply chain is highly interconnected with a dependency graph where vulnerabilities in one component can affect multiple parts of the system.
- Attackers can exploit the AI supply chain to manipulate model behavior or extract training data, posing significant risks beyond traditional infrastructure breaches.
- Detecting and defending against such attacks requires advanced techniques like scanning for vulnerabilities in ML frameworks and monitoring unusual activities related to AI tool configurations.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
SANDWORM_MODE is a sophisticated supply chain attack that leverages the trust in AI tools and workflows to infiltrate systems. Adversaries exploit vulnerabilities within third-party software integrations, such as chat installers, to inject malicious code or trojans into cloud environments.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries are embedding malicious code within legitimate AI toolchains to compromise data integrity in cloud-based environments.
- Supply chain attacks targeting AI-augmented workflows are increasingly sophisticated and harder to detect without advanced threat intelligence.
- CrowdStrike's Falcon platform detects new prompt injection techniques that allow adversaries to manipulate AI models for nefarious purposes, such as stealing sensitive information or controlling system operations.
- Third-party software integrations, like chat applications, pose a significant risk due to their extensive network and data access permissions within cloud infrastructures.
