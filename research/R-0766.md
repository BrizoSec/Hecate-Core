## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
The August 2026 infostealer landscape is characterized by high-volume distribution of families such as Remus, Vidar, LummaC2, and ACRStealer through cracked software disguises, SEO poisoning, and file-hosting platforms like Mega and Mediafire. These threats primarily utilize EXE execution (97.3%) with occasional DLL side-loading techniques involving legitimate libraries like python37.dll to evade detection while impersonating entities such as Microsoft Corporation.

### Key Findings
- Remus was the most frequently detected infostealer in August 2026, distributed alongside Vidar, LummaC2, and ACRStealer via crack software disguises and search engine optimization (SEO) poisoning strategies on platforms like Mega and Mediafire.
- Malicious actors predominantly impersonated Microsoft Corporation to lend credibility to their disguises, with other observed impersonations including Blue Ridge Solutions, Oleg N. Scherbakov, Frost Union Networks, and Cedar Stone Collective.
- DLL side-loading constituted approximately 2.7% of execution types, specifically leveraging legitimate files such as python37.dll, python36.dll, Lucene.Net.dll, OLEACC.dll, msado15.dll, and Microsoft.Data.Edm.dll to execute malicious payloads.
- Email campaigns delivered Formbook disguised as Turkish bank transaction statements and AgentTesla disguised as business quote requests from Indian plumbing companies, with the latter utilizing SMTP for data exfiltration.
- Exploitation of the Renpy game development tool was identified as a distribution vector, where a ZIP file containing setup.exe and setup.py links to execute malicious scripts that launch ACRStealer.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Infostealer campaigns in August 2026 heavily leverage DLL side-loading with legitimate libraries (e.g., python37.dll) and RenPy game engine exploitation to deploy Remus, Vidar, LummaC2, and ACRStealer while evading signature-based detection. Adversaries utilize SEO poisoning and impersonation of Microsoft Corporation to distribute payloads via file-hosting platforms, with execution predominantly occurring through EXE files and specific parent-child process chains involving setup.exe.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use DLL side-loading with legitimate Python libraries and RenPy exploitation to execute infostealers (Remus, LummaC2, ACRStealer) on endpoints disguised as cracked software or SEO-optimized downloads.

### Gaps Identified
- Lack of visibility into `process.cmd_line` parameters and parent-child process relationships hinders detection of the setup.exe launching setup.py chain used in RenPy exploitation.
- Inability to distinguish between legitimate and malicious DLL loading events without behavioral baselining allows python37.dll/python36.dll side-loading to bypass standard executable hashes.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use DLL side-loading with legitimate Python libraries and RenPy exploitation to execute infostealers (Remus, LummaC2, ACRStealer) on endpoints disguised as cracked software or SEO-optimized downloads.
- Gap: Lack of visibility into `process.cmd_line` parameters and parent-child process relationships hinders detection of the setup.exe launching setup.py chain used in RenPy exploitation.
- Gap: Inability to distinguish between legitimate and malicious DLL loading events without behavioral baselining allows python37.dll/python36.dll side-loading to bypass standard executable hashes.
- Focus: Monitor for anomalous process creation involving `python*.dll` or `RenPy` executables, specifically tracking parent processes initiating child processes with suspicious command-line arguments.
