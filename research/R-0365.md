### Summary
Adversaries may exploit the dynamic risk-based model of CrowdStrike Falcon Platform by focusing on vulnerabilities that are not prioritized due to lower CVSS scores but still have significant business impact. They could also manipulate attack paths or timing to evade detection and remediation efforts.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries target vulnerabilities with low CVSS scores but high business impact, exploiting the risk-based prioritization model of Falcon Platform.
- Attackers use sophisticated techniques to alter attack vectors and timing to avoid detection by the platform's dynamic scoring system.
- The integration of third-party EDR tools like Microsoft Defender can introduce additional entry points for attackers to exploit.
- Adversaries leverage known exploits and IoAs (Indicator of Attack) to circumvent Falcon Platform’s Next-Gen SIEM capabilities, aiming to establish persistence within targeted networks.
- CrowdStrike's Falcon Onum use cases highlight the importance of continuous monitoring and analysis to detect advanced threats that may bypass traditional patching methods.
- Federal agencies must maintain robust visibility and detection mechanisms alongside CrowdStrike solutions to fully mitigate sophisticated adversary tactics.
