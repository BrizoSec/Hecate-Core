## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
SocGholish is a malware framework active since at least 2017 that utilizes social engineering and drive-by download tactics, often disguising itself as legitimate software updates to install payloads without user consent. It primarily delivers malicious code through JavaScript embedded in compromised websites or via HTML smuggling, enabling the automatic execution of arbitrary commands and exfiltration of sensitive data.

### Key Findings
- The malware employs obfuscation techniques and JavaScript-based delivery methods to automate the download and execution of secondary payloads, including backdoors, from command-and-control servers over HTTPS on port 443.
- SocGholish is historically linked to multiple threat groups, including Evil Corp, TA569, Gold Drake, and UNC2165, indicating its use as a versatile loader for distributing various malicious tools such as RansomHub ransomware.
- The framework leverages Trojanized Download Sites (TDS) throughout the infection process to redirect users and facilitate the silent push of malware components after initial contact with infected websites.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are conducting a targeted phishing campaign against Washington State election infrastructure using electorally themed emails to deliver the SocGholish framework, which profiles victim systems and downloads secondary payloads. The attackers aim to establish initial access to voter database networks and conduct reconnaissance for potential DDoS attacks to undermine public confidence in the upcoming November elections.

### Key Findings
- The campaign utilizes malicious zip attachments or links to look-alike domains (e.g., wa-elections-support.com) impersonating the Washington Secretary of State's office to deliver SocGholish.
- SocGholish executes obfuscated WScript commands to download secondary payloads and achieves persistence by creating scheduled tasks named "UpdateCheck" and modifying registry run keys.
- The malware employs JavaScript profiling and HTML smuggling techniques to profile victim systems before exfiltrating data or deploying further tools.
- Threat actors are actively reconning election websites for potential DDoS vectors intended to overwhelm servers and erode public trust on election day.
- Historical context indicates SocGholish has been active since 2017, often leveraging compromised legitimate websites and social engineering disguised as software updates.
