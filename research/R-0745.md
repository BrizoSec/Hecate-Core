## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
T-Mobile does not operate a points-based reward system, making any text messages claiming rewards points are expiring inherently fraudulent. These smishing attempts utilize social engineering tactics, such as urgency and fabricated balances, to direct victims to phishing sites designed to harvest credentials or payment details.

### Key Findings
- The absence of a T-Mobile rewards points program indicates that any SMS regarding point expiration is a scam rather than legitimate system telemetry.
- Phishing infrastructure utilizes rotating domains (e.g., .top extensions) mimicking the 't-mobile' subdomain to evade detection and mimic legitimate branding.
- Attackers employ high-volume, semantically similar message templates with superficial variations in salutations, dates, and point balances to bypass content filters.
- The campaign relies on social engineering urgency, citing specific imminent expiry dates and arbitrary point balances (e.g., 18,400 points) to compel immediate clicking of malicious links.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are conducting a large-scale smishing campaign targeting T-Mobile customers with fraudulent messages claiming that rewards points (typically 18,400) are imminently expiring. These messages use social engineering tactics to create urgency and direct recipients to click links hosted on rotating, impersonating domains to steal login credentials and personal information.

### Key Findings
- The campaign originated in early May 2026 and utilized over 1,000 semantically similar message templates with superficial variations such as salutations, dates, and point balances.
- Attackers employed at least 81 rotating domains following a specific pattern (e.g., 't-mobile.[random].top') to host phishing pages designed to mimic legitimate T-Mobile websites.
- Messages use generic greetings like 'Dear Customer' rather than personalized names, relying on formal language and the fear of losing valuable points to bypass skepticism.
- The operation experienced two major spikes in detection activity before declining, with domains being short-lived and replaced frequently to evade blocks.
- The primary objective is social engineering to trick recipients into divulging T-Mobile login credentials, personal details, or payment information via the phishing links.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
A large-scale smishing campaign utilizes over 1,000 semantically similar SMS templates claiming imminent T-Mobile rewards points expiry to drive victims to rotating 't-mobile.[random].top' phishing domains. Technical detection relies on correlating outbound DNS queries for these specific domain patterns with HTTP connections from user agents that initiate credential harvesting attempts.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use high-volume smishing messages with fabricated urgency to redirect users to rotating subdomain infrastructure designed to harvest login credentials and payment details.

### Gaps Identified
- Lack of native carrier-level SMS metadata telemetry prevents direct correlation of the initial social engineering vector before endpoint interaction occurs.
- DNS logs may capture the resolution but fail to distinguish between benign lookups and active credential-harvesting sessions without concurrent HTTP User-Agent or POST request analysis.

### Key Findings
