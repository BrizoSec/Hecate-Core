## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
The 2026 Microsoft Digital Defense Report indicates that threat actors are increasingly integrating AI into reconnaissance, social engineering, and exploit development to achieve greater speed and scale while relying on familiar methods like identity compromise. Adversaries are specifically leveraging AI agents and compromised service principals to interact with enterprise infrastructure, making the interconnectedness of systems a critical factor in both launching attacks and defending against them.

### Key Findings
- Threat actors are incorporating AI into multiple stages of the attack lifecycle, including reconnaissance, social engineering, malware development, and post-compromise activity, primarily to increase the speed and scale of operations while tailoring campaigns more efficiently.
- Adversaries are exploiting 'agentic' capabilities, where AI agents interact with enterprise data, applications, and APIs, as seen in incidents like Storm-3168 which utilized compromised service principals for cloud attacks.
- The security posture of AI systems is dependent on the broader environment, including the identities, permissions, data access, and infrastructure surrounding the model, rather than just the model itself.
- AI tools are being used by attackers for vulnerability discovery and exploit development, compressing the time required to identify weaknesses in software before they can be exploited.
- Effective defense requires correlating signals across interconnected systems (endpoints, identities, cloud, applications) because threat activity spanning multiple domains may not be visible when viewed in isolation.
- Trusted sharing of intelligence across organizations and public-private partnerships is essential for connecting disparate pieces of activity that no single organization can observe alone.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Threat actors are leveraging AI agents and automated tools to accelerate the kill chain, specifically compressing vulnerability discovery and exploitation timelines while relying on compromised identities and service principals for lateral movement. Effective detection requires correlating telemetry across endpoints, identities, and cloud APIs to identify the interconnected patterns of agentic behavior that isolated monitoring sources miss.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use AI-driven automation and compromised service principals to rapidly discover vulnerabilities and execute agentic cloud attacks against enterprise infrastructure.

### Gaps Identified
- Inability to correlate non-standard API authentication sequences between autonomous agents and backend services, allowing unmanaged agentic traffic to bypass traditional identity-based anomaly detection.
- Lack of telemetry visibility into the 'memory' state and tool-use context of AI agents, preventing detection of prompt injection or unauthorized data exfiltration within legitimate-looking application workflows.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use AI-driven automation and compromised service principals to rapidly discover vulnerabilities and execute agentic cloud attacks against enterprise infrastructure.
- Gap: Inability to correlate non-standard API authentication sequences between autonomous agents and backend services, allowing unmanaged agentic traffic to bypass traditional identity-based anomaly detection.
- Gap: Lack of telemetry visibility into the 'memory' state and tool-use context of AI agents, preventing detection of prompt injection or unauthorized data exfiltration within legitimate-looking application workflows.
- Focus: Monitor for rapid, high-volume interactions between service principals and cloud resource APIs that deviate from established baseline automation patterns.
- Focus: Correlate endpoint process execution chains with network activity to identify automated malware development or exploit generation scripts driven by AI assistance.
