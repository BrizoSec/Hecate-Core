## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Maltrail is a system designed to detect malware activity based on tracking various indicators of compromise (IOC). It typically monitors network traffic for patterns that are indicative of malicious behavior or known threats.

### Key Findings
- Monitors for specific IP addresses, domain names, and URLs associated with known malicious activities.
- Logs unusual outbound connections, which could indicate data exfiltration or communication with command-and-control servers.
- Uses predefined signatures or heuristics to identify suspicious patterns that deviate from normal network behavior.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries may abuse IP addresses and domain names associated with known malicious activities to establish command-and-control (C2) communications or deliver payloads. This involves monitoring specific indicators of compromise (IOCs) that correlate with malicious traffic patterns, which are likely to evolve by incorporating new techniques such as obfuscation and evasion methods.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries use compromised legitimate services for C2 infrastructure, making detection challenging due to the blending of benign and malicious network traffic.
- [UNCERTAIN] The integration of AI-driven malware is expected to increase in sophistication, potentially incorporating techniques from established malware frameworks but with enhanced evasion capabilities against traditional detection methods.
- Common tools such as PowerShell and batch scripts continue to be favored for executing payloads on compromised systems due to their native presence on Windows machines and ease of obfuscation.
- The use of domain generation algorithms (DGAs) remains prevalent, allowing adversaries to dynamically generate C2 domains, complicating the process of blocking malicious activity through static IOCs.
- Indicators such as unusual outbound traffic patterns, DNS requests for non-existent or suspicious domains, and unexpected data exfiltration attempts from compromised networks are critical in identifying potential breaches.
- Social engineering tactics remain a primary method for initial compromise, with phishing emails containing malicious attachments or links being the most common vector.
