## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
From the perspective of an endpoint or network sensor, data is typically collected continuously and transmitted to a centralized security platform, where it is processed, stored, and analyzed. However, the system's design often prioritizes data ingestion over export, making retrieval and reuse of data for third-party analysis or migration significantly more complex and costly.

### Key Findings
- Data export from security platforms is often hindered by proprietary access protocols and opaque on-disk data formats, requiring significant engineering effort to extract usable data.
- Exiting a security vendor platform involves substantial migration costs, including 400 to 800+ engineering hours for rule migration and a 47-day detection gap before operational parity is achieved.
- Managed SIEM solutions lock organizations into vendor-specific detection logic and institutional knowledge, increasing switching costs and reducing flexibility to adopt best-fit technologies.
- Data egress fees and 'data gravity' in cloud environments create financial disincentives to migrate, even when better or cheaper alternatives exist.
- The cost of vendor lock-in extends beyond licensing, affecting governance, data ownership, and long-term strategic agility due to restricted data access and export capabilities.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries do not directly exploit data access limitations caused by security vendor lock-in, but the resulting technical and organizational constraints can indirectly benefit them by reducing an organization's ability to respond to threats. The inability to easily extract and analyze security data across platforms can delay threat detection, hinder incident response, and limit the reuse of custom detection logic, thereby increasing the window of opportunity for adversaries to operate undetected.

### Key Findings
- Vendor lock-in creates barriers to data portability, making it difficult to export security data, especially custom correlation rules and business context, which are critical for effective detection and response.
- Egress fees and proprietary data formats increase the cost and complexity of moving data out of a security platform, effectively discouraging migration and reinforcing dependency on a single vendor.
- Organizations may become technically entrenched due to specialized training and integration with a single vendor’s ecosystem, reducing agility in adopting better tools or responding to evolving threats.
- Contractual commitments such as minimum-use agreements can prevent organizations from switching vendors even when better options exist, increasing long-term risk and reducing operational flexibility.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Security vendor lock-in creates significant technical barriers to data export through proprietary formats, egress fees, and complex migration requirements, which indirectly enable adversary persistence by delaying detection and response. These constraints reduce SOC agility and hinder cross-platform analysis, increasing the time-to-detect for malicious activity. The inability to extract and reuse custom detection logic limits defensive adaptability in real-time threat hunting.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use prolonged dwell time enabled by data export restrictions to maintain stealthy persistence, leveraging vendor-specific detection silos that prevent cross-platform correlation and timely response.

### Gaps Identified
- Lack of standardized, vendor-agnostic data export APIs prevents real-time extraction and analysis of historical telemetry, limiting the ability to detect adversarial patterns across environments.
- Proprietary on-disk data formats and lack of schema transparency prevent automated rule migration and replay of detection logic, reducing the effectiveness of cross-platform threat hunting.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use prolonged dwell time enabled by data export restrictions to maintain stealthy persistence, leveraging vendor-specific detection silos that prevent cross-platform correlation and timely response.
- Gap: Lack of standardized, vendor-agnostic data export APIs prevents real-time extraction and analysis of historical telemetry, limiting the ability to detect adversarial patterns across environments.
- Gap: Proprietary on-disk data formats and lack of schema transparency prevent automated rule migration and replay of detection logic, reducing the effectiveness of cross-platform threat hunting.
- Focus: Develop and validate automated data export pipelines using open formats (e.g., JSON, Parquet) to enable rapid retrieval and reuse of SIEM data across platforms.
- Focus: Implement telemetry normalization frameworks that map vendor-specific event structures to a common schema, enabling cross-platform correlation and detection reuse.
- Focus: Establish baseline metrics for data egress latency and migration complexity to identify when operational agility is compromised by vendor lock-in
