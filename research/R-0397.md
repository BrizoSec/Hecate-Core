## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries may exploit the openness of AI models and shared tools within the Open Secure AI Alliance by injecting malicious data or code that could compromise the integrity of these systems. This could enable them to evade detection mechanisms designed for defensive purposes, thereby undermining collective defense efforts.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries can leverage open-source AI frameworks to inject backdoors or manipulate machine learning models during training phases, leading to biased outcomes that favor their malicious objectives.
- By participating in the development and testing of shared security tools, threat actors could introduce vulnerabilities or deploy false updates designed to subvert these tools' intended defensive capabilities.
- Adversaries might use social engineering tactics to gain access to sensitive data or credentials necessary for modifying open AI models and harnesses used by the alliance members.
- The reliance on open collaboration can be exploited through distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks targeting shared infrastructure, disrupting collaborative efforts aimed at enhancing AI security.
