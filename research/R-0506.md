## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries may abuse IP cameras in Ukraine to establish a persistent presence within networks, leveraging the devices' inherent connectivity and potentially vulnerable configurations. This allows for surveillance, data exfiltration, or as a pivot point for lateral movement.

### Key Findings
- IP camera firmware can be exploited or misconfigured to provide remote access to adversaries, enabling them to monitor network traffic and capture sensitive information.
- [UNCERTAIN] Adversaries might employ default credentials or brute-force attacks to gain unauthorized access to IP cameras, escalating privileges within the network.
- Once compromised, these devices can serve as command-and-control (C2) servers for further malicious activities due to their built-in connectivity features and lesser scrutiny compared to standard IT infrastructure.
- IP camera networks may be isolated from critical systems but could still act as a beachhead into more secure parts of the network through lateral movement techniques such as pass-the-hash or SMB relay attacks.
- The presence of unusual outbound connections from IP cameras to known malicious IPs or domains can indicate exfiltration activities or C2 communications.
- Regular firmware updates and security patches for IP camera systems are critical in mitigating risks associated with known vulnerabilities that adversaries could exploit.
