## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
A malicious Chinese-language executable, masquerading as a resume from a Beijing Institute of Technology graduate student, triggers a multi-stage attack chain using a custom Go loader with sandbox evasion and sleep-timer techniques. The loader downloads a legitimate DOCX decoy, then executes a 1,454-byte SNOWLIGHT stager that connects to infrastructure, downloads 4.65 MB of XOR-decoded data (key 0x99), and launches a fileless VShell RAT. The payload enables command execution, file access, screen capture, and lateral movement, targeting academics in electrical engineering, power systems, or AI research in mainland China.

### Key Findings
- The initial payload is a fileless Go loader that performs sandbox detection, CPU checks, and sleep-timer evasion before proceeding with the infection chain.
- A 1,454-byte SNOWLIGHT stager is executed in memory, connecting to infrastructure at http://38.207.178.192:50813 and downloading 4.65 MB of data that is XOR-decoded using key 0x99.
- The final stage is a fileless VShell RAT, which provides persistent access with capabilities including command execution, file access, screen capture, and lateral movement.
- Network indicators include connections to the domains d.d.edsxhbba06.shop, oa.muxmyee520.shop, pan.qlam.cc, v.n.1.xcwanmei09.shop, and www.qlam.cc, and to the URLs http://38.207.178.192:50813/EasyConnectUpdata_Log.txt and http://38.207.178.192:50813/MySQL_LOG.txt.
- The use of panel-generated SNOWLIGHT components in cracked VShell releases suggests the actor is leveraging widely available commodity tooling, making definitive attribution unreliable beyond the China-oriented academic targeting.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Adversaries employ a fileless, multi-stage infection chain leveraging a custom Go loader with sandbox evasion and sleep-timer techniques to deliver a VShell RAT via a SNOWLIGHT stager, using a deceptive academic lure targeting Chinese researchers in defense-related technical fields. The attack relies on memory-resident execution, XOR-decoded payloads (key 0x99), and domain-based C2 infrastructure to maintain persistence and evade detection.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use a custom Go loader with sandbox evasion and sleep-timer techniques to execute a fileless SNOWLIGHT stager in memory, which downloads and XOR-decodes a 4.65 MB payload (key 0x99) to deploy a VShell RAT on targeted academics in China’s defense-tech sector.

### Gaps Identified
- The use of widely available, panel-generated SNOWLIGHT components obscures actor-level attribution and complicates detection based on unique payload signatures, as the stager and C2 infrastructure are indistinguishable from legitimate or open-source tooling.
- Fileless execution via memory-resident payloads (e.g., SNOWLIGHT stager and VShell RAT) lacks direct file-based telemetry, making detection dependent on behavioral indicators such as process injection, anomalous network traffic, and suspicious command-line patterns—many of which are not consistently logged or correlated.
