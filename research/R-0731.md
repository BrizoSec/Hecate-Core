## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Mythic is an open-source, cross-platform command-and-control framework that utilizes a web-based operator interface and Docker-based deployment to manage agents communicating via configurable C2 profiles. Normal operation involves agents connecting to the Mythic server through defined transport methods, often defaulting to identifiable artifacts like specific TLS certificate subjects and Nginx responses on port 7443.

### Key Findings
- Default deployments expose distinct fingerprinting artifacts, including TLS certificates with organization subject 'O=Mythic' or 'O=Mythic C2' and consistent Nginx HTTP responses on port 7443.
- The framework supports modular transport profiles that define network protocols and message framing, allowing agents to link beacons together to build chains deep into a network.
- Publicly observable infrastructure shows concentrations in DigitalOcean, AWS, and Azure environments, with geographic clustering in the United States, Hong Kong, and China.
- Operational clusters are distinguished from training environments by the use of custom domains and staged payloads, alongside specific transport mechanisms like Discord-based channels and steganography.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries deploy the open-source Mythic command-and-control framework on public internet hosts, often utilizing default configurations that facilitate detection through specific TLS certificates and network ports. This infrastructure is predominantly hosted on major cloud providers in the United States, Hong Kong, and China, with usage patterns distinguishing between training environments and operational deployments involving advanced evasion techniques.

### Key Findings
- Detection is facilitated by identifying default deployment artifacts, including TLS certificates with 'O=Mythic' subjects, responses on port 7443, and internal PKI chains.
- Infrastructure spans predominantly DigitalOcean, AWS, and Azure environments, with a geographic concentration in the United States, Hong Kong, and China.
- Host-based detection indicators include Docker Compose deployments featuring container names such as 'mythic_nginx', 'mythic_rabbitmq', or 'mythic_server'.
- Operational use cases are distinguished from training environments by isolated deployments utilizing custom domains, staged payloads, Discord-based transports, and steganographic techniques.
- Network-based detection should alert on outbound connections to port 7443 directed at dedicated VPS or cloud IPs lacking known legitimate services.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Adversaries are deploying Mythic C2 infrastructure at internet scale, primarily on DigitalOcean, AWS, and Azure hosts in the US, Hong Kong, and China. Detection relies on identifying default deployment artifacts like TLS certificates with 'O=Mythic' subjects, Nginx responses on port 7443, and specific Docker container names, while distinguishing operational clusters from training environments via custom domains and advanced transport methods.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use Mythic C2 framework with default TLS certificates and port 7443 exposure to establish command-and-control infrastructure on public cloud providers for unauthorized intrusions.

- Lack of telemetry to distinguish between legitimate red team training environments and active adversary operations based solely on static infrastructure artifacts.
- Inability to detect advanced evasion techniques such as Discord-based transports or steganographic payloads without endpoint or deep packet inspection capabilities.
