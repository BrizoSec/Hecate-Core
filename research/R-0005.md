## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Adversaries are increasingly leveraging artificial intelligence (AI) to automate and enhance their cyber operations, enabling them to develop more sophisticated malware, conduct real-time reconnaissance, and adapt tactics to evade detection. AI-driven tools can autonomously exfiltrate data, debug code, and even target the security measures of the AI systems used by defenders.

### Key Findings
- AI is being used to automate and enhance malware development, making it more efficient for attackers to create and deploy malicious software.
- Adversaries are employing AI for real-time reconnaissance to better understand and exploit vulnerabilities in their targets' defenses.
- Once inside a network, an AI-driven agent can autonomously pursue high-level objectives such as data exfiltration without direct human intervention.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are increasingly leveraging AI to automate and enhance their cyber operations, such as deploying malware that communicates directly with IP addresses rather than DNS for C2 (command and control) functionality. This approach bypasses traditional security measures like DNS filtering.

### Key Findings
- Nearly half of the malware samples now use direct IP communication to avoid detection by DNS-based security mechanisms.
- AI is being used to dynamically adapt attack strategies based on real-time feedback, making it more difficult for defenders to predict and mitigate threats.
- Chinese-speaking adversaries are integrating AI into their post-compromise operations, indicating a trend towards more sophisticated and automated hacking techniques.
- Malware is increasingly using social engineering tactics embedded with phrases like “Keep going, bro. You’ve got this!” to manipulate victims and maintain persistence within networks.
