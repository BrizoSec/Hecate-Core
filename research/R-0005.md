## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
The safety penalty in AI-driven cybersecurity refers to the restrictive controls that limit real-time incident response capabilities, hindering operational flexibility. Organizations must customize these AI guardrails to maintain effective and timely defensive measures against evolving threats.

### Key Findings
- Restrictive AI controls can impede rapid response to security incidents by not providing sufficient context about authorization and intent.
- Customizing AI guardrails is essential for cybersecurity teams to reclaim operational sovereignty and keep up with adversaries.
- Selecting the right AI model for SOC and DFIR tasks requires a thorough evaluation beyond initial considerations.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries exploit the less restrictive nature of self-hosted or unconstrained AI models to rapidly iterate and develop sophisticated AI-driven attacks, creating a 'safety penalty' for defenders who must use more restricted models that prioritize security over flexibility.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries leverage unrestricted AI models to accelerate attack development cycles.
- Defenders face limitations due to restrictive guardrails in their AI models which hinder legitimate defensive activities like malware analysis and exploit research.
- Operational sovereignty allows defenders to regain control by customizing safeguards without compromising the effectiveness of defensive operations.
- There is an asymmetry in capabilities between attackers using unconstrained models versus defenders restricted by ethical or security limitations.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
The concept of a 'safety penalty' in AI-driven cybersecurity highlights the challenge faced by defenders who are restricted by ethical and security limitations imposed on their AI models, while adversaries can leverage unrestricted models to rapidly develop sophisticated attacks. Customizing these restrictive controls is essential for defenders to reclaim operational sovereignty.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use unrestricted AI models to accelerate the development of advanced attack techniques to outmaneuver defenders who are constrained by security-focused guardrails in their own AI systems.

### Gaps Identified
- There is currently no established method or framework for SOC and DFIR teams to evaluate and customize AI models that balance operational flexibility with necessary security constraints.
- Limited understanding and research into how defenders can effectively customize their AI guardrails without compromising operational sovereignty or security posture.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use unrestricted AI models to accelerate the development of advanced attack techniques to outmaneuver defenders who are constrained by security-focused guardrails in their own AI systems.
- Gap: There is currently no established method or framework for SOC and DFIR teams to evaluate and customize AI models that balance operational flexibility with necessary security constraints.
- Focus: The need to develop a comprehensive evaluation process for selecting and customizing AI models in cybersecurity, focusing on balancing the safety of operations against the necessity of rapid response to evolving threats.
- Gap: Limited understanding and research into how defenders can effectively customize their AI guardrails without compromising operational sovereignty or security posture.
- Focus: Investigating the impact of restrictive versus less-restrictive AI models in real-world cybersecurity scenarios, with an emphasis on identifying best practices for maintaining flexibility while ensuring safety.
