## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries abuse the Remote System Discovery technique (T1018) to gather information about other systems on a network, which aids in identifying potential targets for lateral movement and further compromise. This reconnaissance activity is crucial for threat actors to understand the environment before launching more invasive attacks.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries use remote system discovery techniques such as 'net view' or ping sweeps to enumerate reachable systems on a network.
- The integration of modular tools in MaaS (Malware-as-a-Service) platforms enhances adversaries' ability to customize and automate remote system discovery for efficiency.
- Validating the existence and accessibility of discovered systems through additional probes, such as port scanning and service enumeration, is common practice among attackers.
- Detection of Remote System Discovery activities often involves monitoring network traffic for unusual patterns of ICMP or SMB requests that do not align with normal business operations.

## 3. Telemetry Mapping: OCSF Fields

### Summary
Remote System Discovery involves adversaries attempting to identify other systems within a network that could be used for lateral movement. This activity typically includes scanning or pinging remote hosts and collecting responses indicative of system presence.

### Key Fields
- network.http_request (10% populated): HTTP requests made to external addresses or abnormal internal endpoints that are part of reconnaissance activities.
- network.tcp_syn_sent (95% populated): TCP SYN packets sent to various network addresses, indicating an attempt to establish connections with other systems for discovery purposes.
- process.create (85% populated): Creation of processes like 'ping', 'net view', or custom scripts that query remote hosts for information.
- network.icmp_outbound (60% populated): Outgoing ICMP echo requests which are commonly used in pinging to discover reachable network devices and systems.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Adversaries are enhancing their reconnaissance activities through the integration of modular tools in Malware-as-a-Service (MaaS) platforms, using techniques like remote system discovery to identify potential targets for lateral movement and further compromise. These efforts involve probing network segments with ICMP or TCP SYN requests and utilizing legitimate tools such as 'net view' or custom scripts.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use automated scanning and reconnaissance tools to discover reachable systems on a network, aiming to identify potential targets for lateral movement and exploitation.

### Gaps Identified
- The current detection coverage may not adequately account for the dynamic nature of MaaS modular tools that adversaries can customize for stealthier remote system discovery activities.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use automated scanning and reconnaissance tools to discover reachable systems on a network, aiming to identify potential targets for lateral movement and exploitation.
- Gap: The current detection coverage may not adequately account for the dynamic nature of MaaS modular tools that adversaries can customize for stealthier remote system discovery activities.
- Focus: Enhance monitoring capabilities to detect unusual patterns in ICMP or TCP SYN traffic indicative of scanning, while also looking for signs of process creation related to 'net view' and custom scripts used by attackers for reconnaissance.
