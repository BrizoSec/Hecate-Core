## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
In the second quarter of 2026, phishing and weaponized remote management tools are leading attack vectors in cybercrime. Attackers bypass multi-factor authentication using adversary-in-the-middle (AiTM) techniques and exploit collaboration platforms like Microsoft Teams to deploy ransomware rapidly. Remote access software is frequently abused by threat actors targeting cloud and remote infrastructure.

### Key Findings
- Adversary-in-the-Middle (AiTM) phishing kits are widely used to bypass multi-factor authentication, often leveraging social engineering tactics.
- Phishing attacks on collaboration tools such as Microsoft Teams have significantly increased, with a steady rise in detected incidents throughout Q2 2026.
- Remote access software abuse is prevalent among attackers, enabling them to quickly deploy ransomware and destroy data within targeted environments.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
In Q2 2026, adversaries primarily leveraged phishing attacks combined with the weaponization of legitimate remote management tools to gain initial access and maintain stealth. Phishing campaigns increased significantly, often utilizing QR codes and cloud platforms, while ransomware incidents frequently involved trojanized binaries like MeshAgent or Zoho Assist for persistent control.

### Key Findings
- Phishing attacks were responsible for over half of all Talos IR engagements in Q2 2026, marking a substantial increase from previous quarters.
- Adversaries are increasingly using QR codes and cloud platforms as vectors to deliver malicious payloads, bypassing traditional security measures.
- Legitimate remote management tools (RMM) such as MeshAgent and Zoho Assist were commonly compromised and used to facilitate ransomware attacks by providing stealthy access to target networks.
- Trojanized binaries of RMM tools are being deployed to maintain long-term persistence within victim environments, enabling attackers to exfiltrate data or deploy ransomware undetected.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
In Q2 2026, phishing attacks and the weaponization of legitimate remote management tools emerged as dominant tactics for cyber adversaries to bypass MFA, compromise cloud infrastructure, and deploy ransomware. Phishing campaigns through collaboration platforms and QR codes increased significantly, while compromised RMM tools provided stealthy access.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use phishing attacks combined with weaponized remote management tools to gain initial access, maintain persistence, and rapidly deploy ransomware on corporate networks and cloud environments.

### Gaps Identified
- Current telemetry coverage lacks robust detection mechanisms for the early stages of AiTM attacks that bypass multi-factor authentication through social engineering techniques.
- There is insufficient focus on monitoring and detecting suspicious activities related to compromised legitimate RMM tools used in lateral movement within victim networks.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use phishing attacks combined with weaponized remote management tools to gain initial access, maintain persistence, and rapidly deploy ransomware on corporate networks and cloud environments.
- Gap: Current telemetry coverage lacks robust detection mechanisms for the early stages of AiTM attacks that bypass multi-factor authentication through social engineering techniques.
- Gap: There is insufficient focus on monitoring and detecting suspicious activities related to compromised legitimate RMM tools used in lateral movement within victim networks.
- Focus: Increase research and development efforts into advanced phishing detection capabilities, particularly those targeting collaboration platforms like Microsoft Teams and leveraging QR codes.
- Focus: Enhance the capability to detect and respond to the use of weaponized remote management tools by integrating more granular monitoring around RMM software execution and network communications.
