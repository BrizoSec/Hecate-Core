## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
XMRig is a monero mining malware that leverages stolen or compromised AWS IAM credentials to deploy and operate mining operations within Amazon EC2 and ECS without exploiting system vulnerabilities. The operation involves quick enumeration of service quotas and permissions followed by deployment across targeted services.

### Key Findings
- XMRig uses trusted access credentials rather than exploiting security vulnerabilities to gain entry into systems.
- The campaign focuses on AWS EC2 and ECS, indicating a preference for cloud-based mining operations.
- Threat actors quickly assess the environment's capabilities and permissions before deploying their payloads.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries exploit trusted relationships and vulnerabilities to deploy XMRig for Monero mining, often employing techniques such as lateral movement (e.g., Squiblydoo) and evasion methods like application whitelisting bypass. This allows them to hijack compute resources covertly without detection.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries leverage trusted relationships to gain initial access into victim networks.
- XMRig is used as the primary mining tool, often delivered via lateral movement techniques such as Squiblydoo.
- Techniques like setting autorun keys with regsvr32.exe are employed to evade security controls and maintain persistence.
- Adversaries use a combination of lateral movement and cryptomining activities, indicating multi-stage attack patterns.
- The abuse of trusted relationships suggests a targeted approach to infiltrate networks that have privileged access.
- Network sniffing and other AiTM (adversary-in-the-middle) techniques may be employed to support the deployment of XMRig.
