## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
SocGholish operates as a social engineering framework that initially tricks victims into downloading malicious software disguised as legitimate updates, typically via drive-by downloads or phishing attachments. Once executed, it profiles the victim's system and connects to command-and-control servers to download further payloads such as backdoors, stealers, or ransomware.

### Key Findings
- The malware employs social engineering tactics, disguising itself as browser or software updates to induce users to download malicious packages without consent.
- Execution involves running malicious JavaScript code that facilitates communication with command-and-control servers to retrieve additional payloads like backdoor components and stealer routines.
- SocGholish utilizes obfuscation techniques to evade detection and employs infrastructure strategies such as domain shadowing and domain rotation to maintain access.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are executing a targeted phishing campaign against Washington State election infrastructure using electorally themed emails to deliver the SocGholish malware framework. This attack chain relies on malicious ZIP attachments or links to look-alike domains impersonating the Secretary of State's office to establish initial access and profile victim systems.

### Key Findings
- The campaign specifically targets election officials and political organizations in Washington state ahead of the November elections using look-alike domains such as wa-elections-support.com and secure-voter-wa.org.
- SocGholish (FakeUpdates) is delivered via obfuscated JavaScript from %APPDATA%\Local\Temp, utilizing WScript commands to download secondary payloads after profiling the victim's system.
- Threat actors are establishing persistence through scheduled tasks named 'UpdateCheck' and modifications to registry run keys, with C2 traffic observed on IPs 198.51.100.45 and 203.0.113.88.
- Beyond initial access, the malware aims to compromise networks housing voter registration databases and electronic pollbook systems, while adversaries conduct reconnaissance for potential DDoS attacks to erode public confidence.
