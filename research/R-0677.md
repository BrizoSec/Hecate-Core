## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are leveraging generative AI to execute large-scale phishing campaigns targeting US-based enterprises, utilizing sophisticated executive impersonation and fabricated ServiceNow invoices to request ACH transfers. These attacks employ layered social engineering techniques, including lookalike domains and spoofed email threads, to enhance legitimacy and reduce recipient skepticism.

### Key Findings
- The campaign involved the distribution of over one million phishing emails between August 3-5, primarily targeting US-based organizations (87.7%).
- Attackers utilized lookalike domains 'service-nowinc.com' and 'domainlify.net', with the latter used in Reply-To fields to mimic legitimate ServiceNow infrastructure.
- Fraudulent invoices requested ACH transfers of approximately $50,000 and featured detailed branding, personalized recipient information, and specific payment instructions to attacker-controlled bank accounts.
- The attacks demonstrated AI-assisted indicators such as extensive HTML comments, structured section labeling, and uniform template construction across the phishing emails.
- Defensive strategies recommended include configuring automatic attack disruption in Microsoft Defender XDR, adopting a Zero Trust security model, and strictly verifying user identity and device posture.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Threat actors are executing large-scale, AI-generated phishing campaigns targeting US enterprises by spoofing ServiceNow infrastructure to impersonate C-suite executives and demand immediate ACH transfers. The attack artifacts exhibit distinct generative model fingerprints, including excessive HTML comments, rigid section labeling, and uniform template construction across millions of emails. Detection requires shifting focus from traditional keyword matching to analyzing the structural metadata of email payloads and correlating lookalike domain registrations with outbound financial requests.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use AI-generated HTML structures with excessive comments and structured labeling within spoofed ServiceNow emails to impersonate executives and facilitate ACH fraud on US-based enterprise finance departments.

### Gaps Identified
- Lack of semantic analysis capabilities in standard email gateways to detect the specific 'AI fingerprint' artifacts (e.g., uniform template construction, extensive hidden HTML comments) that distinguish generative content from human-crafted phishing.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use AI-generated HTML structures with excessive comments and structured labeling within spoofed ServiceNow emails to impersonate executives and facilitate ACH fraud on US-based enterprise finance departments.
- Gap: Lack of semantic analysis capabilities in standard email gateways to detect the specific 'AI fingerprint' artifacts (e.g., uniform template construction, extensive hidden HTML comments) that distinguish generative content from human-crafted phishing.
- Focus: Implement telemetry monitoring for newly registered lookalike domains (specifically mimicking service-now[.]com patterns) and correlate their usage with Reply-To header spoofing against internal executive aliases or finance distribution lists.
