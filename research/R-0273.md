## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are leveraging an adversary-in-the-middle (AiTM) attack technique to intercept and modify network traffic in real-time, specifically targeting Siemens ROX II systems with a zero-day exploit. This allows them to redirect SMS login codes and gain unauthorized access to safety instrumented systems.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries are employing AiTM techniques to manipulate network communications between target devices and their legitimate endpoints.
- A specific zero-day vulnerability in Siemens ROX II systems is being exploited, enabling interception of critical security mechanisms like SMS-based two-factor authentication.
- The exploitation of this vulnerability highlights the increased risk posed by unpatched or newly discovered software flaws in industrial control systems (ICS).
- Attackers are demonstrating sophisticated capabilities to exploit real-time network traffic, indicating advanced knowledge and access levels within targeted networks.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Adversaries are exploiting a zero-day vulnerability in Siemens ROX II systems to intercept and manipulate network traffic, particularly targeting SMS-based two-factor authentication mechanisms. The attack involves sophisticated AiTM techniques and leverages the system's reliance on unpatched software flaws for unauthorized access.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use adversary-in-the-middle (AiTM) attacks to exploit zero-day vulnerabilities in Siemens ROX II systems, redirecting SMS login codes to gain unauthorized access to safety instrumented systems and industrial control networks.

### Gaps Identified
- The current telemetry does not fully capture real-time network traffic analysis or modifications that could indicate AiTM activities beyond basic process creation and user authentication monitoring.
- There is a lack of comprehensive threat intelligence on similar attacks targeting other ICS components that might share technological or operational similarities with Siemens ROX II.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use adversary-in-the-middle (AiTM) attacks to exploit zero-day vulnerabilities in Siemens ROX II systems, redirecting SMS login codes to gain unauthorized access to safety instrumented systems and industrial control networks.
- Gap: The current telemetry does not fully capture real-time network traffic analysis or modifications that could indicate AiTM activities beyond basic process creation and user authentication monitoring.
- Focus: Enhance detection capabilities for real-time network manipulation by integrating deep packet inspection (DPI) tools to identify anomalies in SMS traffic patterns and encrypted data exchanges. Additionally, prioritize the development of specific IoCs related to known zero-day vulnerabilities in Siemens ROX II systems.
- Gap: There is a lack of comprehensive threat intelligence on similar attacks targeting other ICS components that might share technological or operational similarities with Siemens ROX II.
- Focus: Expand research and monitoring efforts to include peer systems and protocols commonly used within industrial control environments, such as Modbus, OPC UA, and others. This can help in identifying cross-system attack vectors and enhancing overall security posture.
