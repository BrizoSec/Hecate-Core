## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries abuse the concept of 'Phantom Squatting' by registering AI-generated domain names to mimic legitimate software supply chain domains, thereby enabling them to inject malicious updates or packages into the supply chain. This technique exploits the trust relationships established in software distribution networks.

### Key Findings
- Increased use of AI in generating domain names that closely resemble legitimate ones to deceive users and systems
- Phantom squatting is a growing threat vector, with CrowdStrike reporting an uptick since 2017
- Adversaries leverage the compromised supply chain to distribute malware such as NotPetya through legitimate software updates
- Attackers conduct reconnaissance on IP blocks and vulnerability scanning to identify potential targets in the software supply chain

## 3. Telemetry Mapping: OCSF Fields

### Summary
To detect AI-hallucinated domains as a software supply chain vector, focus on DNS query logs for unusual or unexpected domain names. Look for patterns indicating automated generation and usage of non-existent but plausible-looking domains that employees might inadvertently use due to phishing or social engineering tactics.

### Key Fields
- dns.query.name (15% populated): Tracks the domain names being queried by endpoints, which is crucial for identifying potential phantom squatting activity.
