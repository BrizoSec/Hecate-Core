## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
In trusted communication systems, entities communicate securely and assume each other's identities are verified. This trust can be exploited by attackers who假冒合法身份以访问敏感信息或执行未经授权的操作。

### Key Findings
- 在受信任的通信渠道中，实体之间基于验证的身份进行安全交互
- 攻击者可以通过模仿合法用户来滥用这种信任

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries often exploit trusted communication channels by impersonating legitimate entities or insiders, leveraging the inherent trust within these systems to carry out malicious activities such as phishing, data exfiltration, and command-and-control communications.

### Key Findings
- Impersonation of trusted individuals or organizations is a common tactic used to abuse communication channels.
- [UNCERTAIN] Adversaries may use social engineering techniques along with technical exploits to gain access to legitimate accounts.
- Emails from compromised internal accounts are often leveraged for phishing campaigns targeting other employees within the organization.
- Adversaries might exploit vulnerabilities in email encryption or authentication protocols to masquerade as trusted parties.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Adversaries exploit trusted communication channels by impersonating legitimate entities to carry out malicious activities such as phishing and data exfiltration. They leverage social engineering techniques, technical exploits, and the inherent trust within these systems to abuse identities.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use identity impersonation in trusted communication channels to gain unauthorized access and perform malicious activities on targeted organizations

### Gaps Identified
- The lack of detection mechanisms for identifying social engineering tactics used by adversaries to manipulate the trust within communication channels.
- Inadequate monitoring of vulnerabilities in email encryption or authentication protocols that can be exploited to masquerade as trusted parties.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use identity impersonation in trusted communication channels to gain unauthorized access and perform malicious activities on targeted organizations
- Gap: The lack of detection mechanisms for identifying social engineering tactics used by adversaries to manipulate the trust within communication channels.
- Gap: Inadequate monitoring of vulnerabilities in email encryption or authentication protocols that can be exploited to masquerade as trusted parties.
- Focus: Enhancing detection capabilities for unusual activity in communication logs, such as unexpected emails from compromised internal accounts.
- Focus: Strengthening security measures around email encryption and authentication to prevent adversaries from exploiting these systems.
