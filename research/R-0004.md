## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Maltrail is a network traffic detection system that identifies malicious activity by matching observed data against known indicators of compromise (IoCs). It consists of two main components: the sensor, which monitors and analyzes network packets for suspicious behavior; and the server, which collects and processes alerts from multiple sensors.

### Key Findings
- Maltrail employs a traffic analysis approach that involves sniffing network packets to detect communication with known malicious infrastructure.
- The system is designed to report suspicious activity without directly intervening in the network or blocking traffic.
- It uses publicly available blacklists and static trails compiled from various antivirus resources to identify potential threats.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries abuse the Maltrail system by leveraging supply chain compromises to inject malicious traffic indicators and exploit public-facing applications, while also engaging in credential dumping activities to gain unauthorized access to sensitive data.

### Key Findings
- Supply chain compromise is used as an initial access vector to distribute malware through trusted systems.
- Credential dumping techniques are employed to extract stored credentials from compromised environments.
- Tor DLS onion addresses and contact emails are utilized for command and control (C2) communications, maintaining operational security.
- Public-facing applications are targeted for exploitation, likely to establish a foothold within the network.
