## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Business Email Compromise (BEC) is a sophisticated cyberattack where attackers gain access to business email accounts through phishing, social engineering, or other methods. Once inside, they impersonate trusted contacts and manipulate financial transactions to steal money.

### Key Findings
- Attackers typically start by identifying key targets within an organization and then use various techniques such as credential harvesting via phishing emails to gain access to their email accounts.
- Cybercriminals read through the compromised email accounts to understand the internal communication patterns, trusted contacts, and financial transaction procedures of the company.
- Enabling two-factor authentication (2FA) and scrutinizing all incoming financial requests can significantly reduce the risk of falling victim to BEC attacks.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Business email compromise (BEC) attacks often involve attackers leveraging social engineering tactics to manipulate help desk personnel into resetting passwords, allowing the attackers to redirect payroll deposits. Keyloggers and infostealers are commonly used to gather personal information needed for more effective social engineering.

### Key Findings
- Attackers use keyloggers to capture sensitive login credentials and other personal data from employees.
- Social engineering is a critical component in BEC, often involving the impersonation of known vendors or internal staff members.
- VBA stomping techniques are employed to hide malicious payloads within seemingly benign MS Office documents.
- Cloud accounts have become prime targets for attackers due to their accessibility and rich data repositories.
- The widespread availability of proof-of-concept code on platforms like GitHub lowers the technical barrier for conducting BEC attacks.
- There has been a significant increase in detected threats, indicating heightened sophistication and frequency of BEC attempts.

## 3. Telemetry Mapping: OCSF Fields

### Summary
To detect payroll-related business email compromise (BEC) activities, telemetry should capture unusual financial transactions, changes in payment recipient information, irregular employee benefit payments, and anomalous login patterns indicating account compromises.

### Key Fields

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Business Email Compromise (BEC) attacks targeting payroll systems involve sophisticated social engineering techniques, credential harvesting via phishing, and the use of keyloggers to gather sensitive information. Attackers often manipulate help desk personnel to reset passwords, redirecting payroll deposits to illicit accounts.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use social engineering tactics combined with keylogging malware to gather credentials and personal data, enabling them to impersonate trusted contacts and manipulate financial transactions for monetary gain on targeted organizations.

### Gaps Identified
- The current telemetry mapping lacks comprehensive coverage of cloud account compromises and the exfiltration of sensitive financial data stored in cloud repositories.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use social engineering tactics combined with keylogging malware to gather credentials and personal data, enabling them to impersonate trusted contacts and manipulate financial transactions for monetary gain on targeted organizations.
- Gap: The current telemetry mapping lacks comprehensive coverage of cloud account compromises and the exfiltration of sensitive financial data stored in cloud repositories.
- Focus: Investigating the use of VBA stomping techniques within MS Office documents to deliver keyloggers or infostealers, as well as monitoring for unusual login patterns indicating potential credential harvesting activities.
