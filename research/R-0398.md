## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs) are essential components in critical infrastructure, managing automated processes and control systems. They typically operate on dedicated networks isolated from the internet to prevent unauthorized access; however, when exposed, they become vulnerable to cyberattacks.

### Key Findings
- PLCs are often scanned by threat actors for open ports that allow remote access using legitimate software credentials.
- Attackers manipulate PLC control logic and hide anomalies within the system, making detection challenging.
- The affected sectors include government, water supply, and energy infrastructure, indicating a broad impact on critical services.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries use the Network Service Discovery technique (T1046) to identify vulnerable services and devices within a network, particularly targeting PLCs in critical U.S. infrastructure by employing port scans and vulnerability assessments.

### Key Findings
- Adversaries conduct extensive network reconnaissance using tools such as Nmap for discovering open ports and services on remote hosts.
- Vulnerability scanners like Nessus or OpenVAS are utilized to probe the discovered services for known vulnerabilities, especially in industrial control systems (ICS).
- Common indicators of T1046 include unusual outbound traffic from internal networks directed at specific port ranges typically associated with ICS/SCADA protocols.
- Adversaries frequently target legacy PLC systems due to their outdated software and lack of regular security updates, making them more susceptible to exploitation.

## 3. Telemetry Mapping: OCSF Fields

### Summary
Telemetry for capturing network service discovery activities typically involves monitoring network traffic, logs, and system events that indicate an adversary scanning for open ports or services. This includes data from firewall rules, intrusion detection systems (IDS), SIEM alerts, and endpoint security solutions.

### Key Fields
- network_flow_data.src_port: The source port of the initiating host can provide clues about scanning behavior if combined with destination port information.
- network_flow_data.dst_port: Destination ports that are frequently queried or in ranges commonly used for services (e.g., 21-25, 80, 443, 53) may indicate a scan for open services.
- syslog.message: Logs from the system's syslog service can capture events related to network scanning and service discovery attempts. Look for messages containing keywords like 'scan', 'ping', or specific port numbers being queried.
- endpoint_security.process_name: Monitoring process names associated with tools used for network discovery, such as Nmap, Nessus, or custom scripts, can help detect reconnaissance activities.

## 5. Research Synthesis

### Executive Summary
Adversaries are actively using network service discovery techniques to scan and exploit Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs) in critical U.S. infrastructure, leading to potential disruptions in government, water supply, and energy sectors.

### Recommended Hypothesis
> Adversaries use Network Service Discovery to identify vulnerable PLCs and ICS/SCADA services for exploitation on critical U.S. infrastructure.

### Gaps Identified
- There is a lack of visibility into the specific tools and tactics used by adversaries after initial network discovery, such as post-exploitation behavior or lateral movement within the network.
- Current detection mechanisms may not effectively identify and respond to stealthy scanning techniques that blend in with normal traffic patterns.

### Key Findings
- Hypothesis: Adversaries use Network Service Discovery to identify vulnerable PLCs and ICS/SCADA services for exploitation on critical U.S. infrastructure.
- Gap: There is a lack of visibility into the specific tools and tactics used by adversaries after initial network discovery, such as post-exploitation behavior or lateral movement within the network.
- Gap: Current detection mechanisms may not effectively identify and respond to stealthy scanning techniques that blend in with normal traffic patterns.
- Focus: Enhancing endpoint security solutions to detect and block known reconnaissance tools like Nmap and Nessus when they are used from suspicious IP addresses or internal hosts.
- Focus: Implementing more granular firewall rules and intrusion detection systems (IDS) to monitor and alert on unusual outbound scanning activities targeting critical ICS/SCADA port ranges.
