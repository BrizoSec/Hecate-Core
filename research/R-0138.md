## 1. System Research: How It Works

### Summary
Quick Assist is a feature in Microsoft Teams and Windows that allows users to remotely assist other users by sharing their desktop, control applications, or troubleshoot issues. Threat actors are exploiting this functionality through social engineering tactics like vishing (voice phishing) to gain unauthorized access for ransomware distribution.

### Key Findings
- Quick Assist can be activated via a phone call where the attacker requests remote assistance from the victim.
- Microsoft Teams is also being exploited by threat actors who use social engineering techniques to phish users into enabling remote access features.
- Once access is granted, attackers may deploy GoGRPC backdoor malware to maintain persistence and control over compromised systems.

## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are exploiting Microsoft Teams' Quick Assist feature for unauthorized remote session viewing and using the GoGRPC backdoor technique to maintain persistent access. These methods enable them to bypass security measures and stealthily control systems.

### Key Findings
- Abuse of Microsoft Teams Quick Assist for remote desktop sessions without proper authorization.
- Utilization of GoGRPC as a covert communication channel and persistence mechanism within compromised networks.
- Misuse of legitimate software features like Teams Vishing to conduct social engineering attacks.
- Persistence achieved through backdoors that evade detection by maintaining encrypted, low-profile communications.
