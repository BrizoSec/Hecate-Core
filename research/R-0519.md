## 2. Adversary Tradecraft: Attack Techniques

### Summary
Adversaries are increasingly leveraging email phishing campaigns to distribute malware or establish initial footholds within networks. Once inside, they use credential harvesting techniques such as keylogging and password spray attacks to escalate privileges and move laterally across the network.

### Key Findings
- Phishing emails containing malicious attachments or links to compromised websites are common vectors for deploying malware like backdoors or RATs (Remote Access Trojans).
- [UNCERTAIN] Some APT groups may now be using more sophisticated social engineering tactics, such as impersonating high-level executives in the target organization to gain trust and authorization.
- Credential dumping tools are frequently employed by adversaries to extract credentials from memory on compromised machines, allowing them to reuse these credentials for further network infiltration.
- Adversaries often use common tools like PsExec or WMI (Windows Management Instrumentation) to execute commands remotely on other systems within the network without leaving obvious signs of malicious activity.
- The establishment of command and control (C2) communication channels is a critical step in APT operations, usually involving encrypted tunnels through standard ports to avoid detection by security solutions.
- Lateral movement often involves leveraging built-in Windows tools or exploiting vulnerabilities that allow for privilege escalation from one compromised machine to another.
